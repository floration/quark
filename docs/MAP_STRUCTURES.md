# Why every structure is there

Every structure on a map should earn its place: either for the game (cover, a route, a perch, a loop, a sightline breaker) or for the place (it explains what the map is). If neither applies, it goes, or it is replaced by something that does both.

Maps are built by `tools/make_maps.py`. The comments there say the same thing next to the code. Scores are in [MAP_REPORT.md](MAP_REPORT.md).

## Anvil (ranked, all three ladders)
- **The anvil**, with a horn at both ends: the centrepiece. You stand on it to control the middle, or fight around it.
- **Lava troughs**: lethal edges in the lanes either side of the anvil. You get punished for being knocked back carelessly.
- **Galleries at 3.5 m with two ladders**: a second level over each side. The ladders are the slow, exposed way up; the stairs are the safe one.

## Bastion (every mode, built for vs): a castle
- **Curtain wall with a walk on top (7 m), merlons**: the high ring. The merlons are cover from the fields side; the inner lip keeps the walk readable.
- **Towers at corners, flanks and gates**: fighting platforms higher than the wall. Each has doorways onto the walk, so it is a node, not a dead end.
- **Gatehouses with portcullises**: where the teams start, shut in behind bars, so nobody spawn-kills through the gate.
- **The hoarding (timber gallery) on posts**: widens the wall-walk and roofs a covered ring of courtyard below, so there are indoor and outdoor fights.
- **Timber breastwork on the gallery edge, in panels**: archers' cover over the courtyard. It is a wallbang, so it hides you but does not make you safe. The gaps between panels let you drop down.
- **Stairs up to the walls (one flight per wall)**: the way up that isn't a grapple. They are point-symmetric for fairness.
- **The keep on its island, the moat, four drawbridges**: the hill for king of the hill. Four bridges are four ways in. The moat kills, so the bridges are chokepoints you can be knocked off. The **keep ladder** is a slow, exposed way onto its top.
- **Inner ward walls, mid-lane walls, the well, the hay cart**: break the courtyard into lanes and give cover in the open ward.
- **Banners and torches**: light and colour on the big walls. They also tell you which side you are on.
- **The water moat outside and the fields**: scenery. They are the reason the castle walls look like walls and not a box.

## Canal (neutral payload)
- **Causeway with the cart track**: the payload line down the middle.
- **Canals of water either side**: lethal, so flanks are a risk.
- **Three flat bridges per canal**: rotations between bank and causeway.
- **Humpback footbridges**: a second, higher way over each canal. They make a loop with the flat bridges and give a mid-height firing spot.
- **Lock gates with ladders up the posts**: the centrepiece at both ends. The gate beam is a narrow walkway 7 m over the water: a sniper perch you can be knocked off.
- **Canal-side houses**: indoor fights on the banks, and a reason the banks exist.
- **Boathouse canopies**: a roofed fight right beside the open water.
- **Stacked timber on the quays and causeway**: wallbang cover. It stops you but not bullets.
- **Causeway cover blocks**: let the cart pushers survive the open middle.

## Colosseum (every mode, built for tourney)
- **The arena ring with tiered stands**: the stands are levels; the ring is the open floor for tourney fights.
- **Arched inner walls**: break the ring's sightlines into arcs.
- **Arcade roof ring at 4 m with lights and two ladders**: a roofed walkway round the arena. Indoor and outdoor in one map; the ladders are the quick way up from the floor.

## Crucible (duel)
- **The crucible on its island, four narrow bridges**: the centrepiece. Bridges over a lethal moat are where knock-backs win rounds.
- **Molten moat**: lethal, and it glows, so the danger reads at a glance.
- **Roof cross-beams**: grapple anchors over the moat for the grapple-only rounds.
- **Corner pillars with two ladders**: tall pillars for grapple rounds. The two ladders, one per side, turn two of them into 9 m perches over the moat.
- **Flank walls, raised corner ledges with stairs**: cover and a low second level on each side.
- **Timber barricades in front of the spawns**: wallbang cover at the start. You can hide behind it, but it won't stop a shot.

## Depot (bomb)
- **The wall splitting the sites, the defender perch**: the defenders' setup: two sites you can't see both of at once, and a raised spot to hold from.
- **West: the warehouse with racks, its ladder and roof**: the indoor lane. The roof is an overwatch perch over it, and an exposed one.
- **Middle: the freight truck with crates up onto its roof, the windscreen (wallbang)**: the open-yard lane. The truck is the only big cover; its roof is the high ground.
- **East: the raised dock, sunken trench, loading canopy**: the low lane. It fights under a roof, lit from above.
- **The gatehouse at the attackers' entry**: a roofed spot to stack up before the push.
- **Perimeter fence, floodlights**: why the yard is a yard. The lights make the corners readable.

## Foundry (every mode): an indoor factory
- **Sawtooth roof with skylights, ribbed walls, loading bay shutters**: what makes it a factory hall; the skylights light it.
- **Steel column grid**: cover in a huge open floor, and grapple anchors.
- **Crane rails, travelling cranes and hooks**: grapple anchors everywhere overhead. The hooks are swing points.
- **The casting pit and the ladle**: the lethal middle and the centrepiece.
- **Conveyors and presses**: low cover in lanes across the floor.
- **Tanks in the corners**: corner cover, and the corners stop being empty.
- **Catwalk ring at 6 m with bridges, offices**: the second level. The bridges cross the middle and give loops; the offices are small rooms to fight through.

## Furnace (ranked laser vampire)
- **The furnace and its charging deck, two stokers' ladders**: the hot middle. The deck is a 5 m perch reached only by the two exposed ladders, one per side.
- **Molten trenches**: lethal edges in a compact map. Laser fights are about positioning, and here bad positioning kills.
- **Inner walls with doorways**: break the room into lanes at laser range (under 32 m).
- **Roofed side bays (where each player starts)**: the indoor half of the map. Fights move between dark covered bays and the bright furnace hall.
- **Raised side blocks with stairs**: a low second level on each flank.

## Gantry (ranked instagib)
- **Catwalks along both walls, the bridge over the loading bay**: long sightlines on two levels, which is what instagib wants.
- **The open loading bay (no floor)**: lethal; the bridge over it is the risky shortcut.
- **Two overhead cranes with cabs and hanging containers**: grapple points and cover in the air. The cab windows are wallbangs.
- **Floor blocks and the tall pillar**: ways to break line of sight in a map built on sightlines.
- **Roofed loading sheds at both ends**: where each player starts. A dim covered end to fight out of, with the bright open hall between.

## Hamlet (hide and seek / tag)
- **Houses with doors, flat roofs with ladders, pitched roofs**: every building has a way in. Flat roofs are hiding perches; pitched ones you can't stand on.
- **The church and bell tower (ladders)**: the tallest landmark, and a long climb to a hiding spot that is also a dead end.
- **The two-storey house, the barn and hayloft**: indoor hiding on two floors.
- **The windmill hill (terraces), water tower, lookout tower**: vertical spots spread around the valley. The lookout sees a lot; the windmill hill is a maze of tiers.
- **Pond (shallow water), creek with footbridges**: landmarks. You can wade the pond; the creek is crossed at the bridges.
- **Orchard, hedges, haystacks, hay carts**: places to crouch.
- **Garden sheds and woodpiles (wallbangs)**: hiding spots a seeker can shoot through.
- **Dry-stone field walls and log piles (placed on open ground)**: the valley floor was open grass with nowhere to break line of sight. These pieces give it some.

## Harbor (free for all)
- **The quay and the sea**: lethal edge along the north.
- **The freighter you can board**: the big landmark, with a walk-through cabin and a deck to fight on.
- **Three piers, the lighthouse with its ladder**: the lighthouse gallery is the classic exposed perch.
- **Containers in rows**: lanes between the shed and the quay.
- **The warehouse, transit shed, customs office with stairs to its roof**: indoor fights and a mid level. The transit shed breaks the long yard sightlines.
- **The quay canopy, the quay clutter (crates, net racks, the harbourmaster's hut)**: they break the 60 m quay into stretches, so it is not one long sniper lane.
- **The dock crane**: a landmark and grapple anchor in the south.

## Railyard (payload)
- **The winding track**: the payload's route.
- **The tunnel**: the roofed leg of the track, a close fight with doors halfway.
- **The station platform with canopy and ticket office**: a raised platform beside the track. The second ways onto it mean it is never a dead end.
- **The footbridge**: a high crossing over the yard.
- **The engine house and terminus shed**: indoor fights at both ends of the push.
- **The locomotive on the siding**: a landmark and big cover.
- **The water tower with a ladder up its down-pipe**: a lookout over the yard, exposed.
- **Freight wagons, brick yard walls, sleeper stacks, buffer stops, crates and cable drums**: these break the open yard into sections. The walls cut the sightlines up the yard; the stacks give cover beside the track, where the cart fight is.

## Summit (king of the hill)
- **Terraces up to the peak, the beacon mast**: the hill is the peak. Each terrace is a tier to fight up through. The stairs turn a corner each tier, so you can't run straight up.
- **The cliff ledge at 4 m on rock pillars, with ramps**: a second ring round the rim. It gives shelter from the peak's sightlines underneath and a way to flank.
- **Mountain huts with ladders**: indoor rooms, and roofs that are flank perches over the terraces.
- **Crevasses**: lethal corners.
- **Boulders, rocks on the peak**: cover on open mountain.
- **Rock strata**: detail on the cliff walls.

## Temple (ranked vanilla)
- **Halls of columns**: cover at every range. The columns are what make it a temple.
- **The sanctum, the altar and the idol**: the centrepiece and the middle fight.
- **Reflecting pools (water) and the bridge**: a lower floor, the landmark either side, and the bridge over them.
- **Roofs over the end halls**: the indoor half; fights move between covered halls and the open sanctum.
- **Wallbang screens**: cover you can shoot through.
- **Offering stones and toppled column drums**: cover in the open courts between the halls.

## Viaduct (race): two loops over a gorge, each leg higher
Every part is there to make you do something, and to stop you skipping it.

**Leg 1 (ground level, north to south):**
- **The start pavilion**: shade over the grid, and a landmark for where the lap begins.
- **The bridge on stone arches**: a straight to build speed.
- **The stepping stones up**: jumps you have to time.
- **The covered lane**: roofed, so you can't grapple over it.
- **The climb, the beam, the grapple gantry**: the beam is narrow footing; the gantry gives the alternative swing.
- **The slalom**: blocks you weave round.
- **Stones down**: more jumps.
- **The run home, the lap gate**: the finish of loop one, under its own pavilion.

**Leg 2 (east edge, 8 to 14 m):**
- **Stairs up to the east deck, another slalom**.
- **The aqueduct gallery**: a roofed run with arches open to the chasm. The roof is why you can't leap or grapple over this stretch from above.
- **The collapse**: stones zig-zagging over the gap, 1.5 m wide with 1.5 m gaps.
- **The east beam**.
- **The corner tower with a ladder up its face**.

**Leg 3 (west edge, 18 to 22 m):**
- **Hurdles**: jump them.
- **The cliff path**: a 1.2 m ledge under a rock overhang. The overhang keeps you on the ledge.
- **The west beam, stairs up to the north turn**.

**Leg 4 (the skyway, 22 to 30 m, down the middle):**
- **Another slalom**.
- **Stepping stones in the sky**: 1.6 m slabs over 1.6–2 m gaps, rising and falling.
- **The last beam, the finish arch**: directly above the start.

**Around the course:**
- **Spandrel walls**: four stone walls right across the gorge, with an arch cut wherever a leg passes. They are what carries a viaduct. They split the gorge into bays, so you see the next stretch rather than the whole course. Nothing goes over them, so every leg has to thread its own arch.
- **Lanterns on posts**: you can see the line of the course at a glance.
- **Rock strata on the walls**: detail on the gorge walls.
- **The river far below**: what cut the gorge. You are back at your checkpoint long before you would reach it.

**The cheese, measured:** the report checks every leg twice. First, how much a dash-jump (16 m) or a grapple pull (45 m) shortens it: none of them by more than 1.08×. Second, what the leg asks of you: 9.4 jumps per 100 m, and 25% of the course on narrow footing. Before this pass the elevated stairs were solid columns down to the ground. One stood across leg 1's beam, so the intended path was blocked and cutting around it was the only way.
