# Next (full, testing builds since 1.19.3)

Every change on `testing` since production 1.19.3, with what changed and why. The release PR renames this file to `changelogs/full/<VERSION>.md`.

## Settings: batch 1, the 4s (owner, 2026-10-30: "go ahead and start working on implementing all of the settings ... score all the setting out of 4"; q ticked all 231 on the scored list)

- **MORE pages.** CONTROLS and VIDEO + AUDIO were full (the VIDEO page's comments already say a row more pushes SOUND into DEVICES), so each gets a MORE page (footer middle, `moreOpen_`, screen paths `/controls/more`, `/video/more`, `--settingstab controls/more`). The rest of 1.19.4's settings go on pages like these instead of squeezing the tabs.
- **Mouse (#4).** `sens_y` (vertical as a multiple of SENSITIVITY), `invert_y`, `zoom_sens` (a multiple of the existing "same feel" zoom scaling, so x1.00 is today's behaviour). Applied in `Game::turn`.
- **Scoreboard toggle (#67).** `score_toggle`: the key flips `scoresOn_` instead of being held.
- **Background cap and mute (#9).** `bg_fps` (default 60, 0 = FPS LIMIT as usual) caps `Game::maxFps()` while the window isn't focused or is minimized (new iconify callback); `bg_mute` sets the master volume to 0 then. Why: an alt-tabbed game running uncapped burns power and heat for nothing; focused play is untouched.
- **Window (#119, #1, #166).** The windowed size and position are saved on every move / resize (not while maximized or fullscreen) and restored at launch if the title bar lands on a monitor that's still there; WINDOW SIZE offers 720p up to 4K that fit the monitor. A display change (DISPLAY or WINDOW SIZE) shows KEEP / REVERT with a 10 s count and reverts by itself (not in the UI test, whose clicks would race the count).
- **Dedicated GPU (#113).** `NvOptimusEnablement` and `AmdPowerXpressRequestHighPerformance` exported from the Windows game, so hybrid laptops use the dedicated GPU; VIDEO > MORE shows GL_RENDERER and hints when it looks integrated.
- **Shadow quality (#3).** `Renderer::setShadowRes` reallocates the shadow map in place (1024 / 2048 / 4096, clamped to the GPU's limit) and updates both programs' texel size; it used to be cfg-only and read at startup.
- **HUD size (#7).** `hud_size` multiplies every HUD element's own SIZE (each element's `k` and `hudFont`). The menus already scale with the window height, so no separate menu scale was added.
- **Crosshair (#13).** `ch_dot` (0 = THICKNESS), `ch_opacity`, `ch_outline_w`, `ch_outline_a`, `ch_wstyle_<weapon>` (a weapon's own style, -1 = the normal one), and COPY / PASTE CODE: the ch_ lines as one `quark-crosshair:` line; pasting reads only ch_ keys, so a code can't change anything else.
- **Hitsounds (#14, #152).** `hit_volume` (0-2), `hit_pack` (-1 = your SOUNDS pack), `headshot_sound` (a higher ding over the hit on a headshot), `kill_sound` (was tied to HITSOUND).
- **Server list (#78).** `browser_sort`, `browser_hide_full`, `browser_hide_empty`, `browser_max_ping`, `fav_servers` (right-click a row; favourites are listed first and never filtered out), `recent_servers` (the last 8 joined; RECENT cycles them into the address field). The list shows 5 rows instead of 6 to make room for the filter row.
- **Race reset (owner, 2026-10-30: "keybind R should restart the map/reset to checkpoint in race/surf modes").** New action RACE RESET (R; bind_version 4 leaves it unbound if R was already taken) sends `racereset`; the server respawns you at your last checkpoint, or with `racereset full` (no checkpoint yet, or the full restart) at the start with the checkpoint count and splits cleared. Nothing new on the wire: it's a player command and the existing EV_SPAWN. Tap or hold (owner, 2026-10-30: "r tapped = checkpoint, r held for 250ms = full restart"; it was a double press): the checkpoint is sent when R is let go before 250 ms, so a tap acts on release, the earliest moment a tap can be told from a hold. A separate FULL RESTART action (MORE KEYS, unbound) sends `racereset full` on press.

## Settings: batch 2, the 3s (first half)

- **Pages.** `moreOpen_` is now a page number: GAMEPLAY > MORE (1 page), CONTROLS > MORE (1), VIDEO + AUDIO > MORE (3, the footer button reads MORE 2 / MORE 3). Screen paths `/gameplay/more`, `/video/more2`, `/video/more3`; CONTROLS gets a third keys sub-page, MORE KEYS (`/controls/keys2`), because the other two key pages have no free rows.
- **Render scale 25-400% (#250).** Was 50-200%. The renderer already capped the target at the GPU's largest texture, so 400% of a 4K window is clamped to what the driver allows rather than failing.
- **Fog (#43).** `fog`: a multiple of the old fixed density 0.0035 (0 = none), set in both the world and model programs (`Renderer::setFog`; the world program keeps it across map changes).
- **Gamma / contrast / saturation (#29).** `gamma`, `contrast`, `saturation`, a `grade()` at the end of the world, model and sky shaders (after tonemapping and gamma encoding), so the HUD and menus aren't touched. At the default (1, 1, 1) it's one uniform compare per pixel and returns unchanged. Why in those shaders: there's no full-screen post pass at render scale 100% with AA off, and adding one would cost a full-screen draw every frame.
- **Video presets (#56).** `video_preset` cycle: LOW (no player shadows, 1024 shadows, no AA, 75% scale, effects 0.6), MEDIUM (shadows 2048, SMOOTH AA), HIGH (4096, MSAA 4x), COMPETITIVE (no shadows, no AA, no fog, effects 0.5, no kill bursts or recoil kick). It sets those settings once; anything changed afterwards stays.
- **Effects (#30, #49).** `fx_opacity` scales every explosion / spark / burst box's alpha (0 = none drawn), `kill_bursts`, `tracers`, `view_recoil` (the gun model's kick; also moves the muzzle point, so shots still leave the gun you see). Muzzle flash and impact sparks were already switches.
- **Colourblind presets (#11).** Two more TEAM COLORS presets: RED-GREEN (blue / yellow-orange) and BLUE-YELLOW (cyan / red). `colorPairClash` simulates protan, deutan and tritan vision (Machado et al. 2009, full severity, linear RGB) and the PLAYER tab warns when the pair's simulated colours are under 0.4 apart (out of 1.73). Checked: cyan / red, blue / orange and both new presets pass; red / green fails for deutans (0.37), white / yellow for tritans (0.29).
- **Enemy model (#15).** `enemy_model` draws every non-friendly player as that model (client only; ranked already forces STANDARD for everyone and is left alone).
- **Sound groups (#33, #174).** `vol_steps` (your footsteps, jumps, landings), `vol_others_steps` (0-2, everyone else's), `vol_weapons` (every gunshot sound), `vol_objective`: the planted bomb now beeps from where it is, in step with the model's blink (every bomb timer / 20 s, at least 0.15 s), reusing the sticky beep a fifth higher. No UI sounds exist yet, so there's no UI volume.
- **Kill feed / chat (#22).** `feed_time` (6 s), `feed_lines` (6), `feed_mine`, `chat_time` (10 s), `chat_lines` (8), `chat_opacity` (not while typing). Chat history (#138): typing shows 12 lines of the 30 kept, scrolled by the wheel or Page Up / Down.
- **Team chat (#41).** New player command `sayteam`: in a team mode the server sends the EV_CHAT only to the sender's team, with `b = 1` so it shows "(TEAM)"; elsewhere it's an ordinary say. Old clients just show such a line without the tag.
- **Keys (#41, #137, #148, #178).** New actions TEAM CHAT, LAST WEAPON (the weapon held before the current one), SPEC NEXT / SPEC PREV (dead or spectating; a click still cycles), HIDE HUD (`hide_hud_crosshair` keeps the crosshair; the scoreboard still works) - all unbound by default. A bind that takes a key from another action shows "<KEY> WAS ON <ACTION>: UNBOUND THERE" with UNDO for 8 s. The bomb objective line and the F3 hints name your bound key (`Game::keyOf`). INFO's key list said F4 for VOTE START, which has been F5 since bind version 3. `testHeld_` (the UI test's held keys) was 32 bits wide while actions passed 32: now 64, with a static_assert. Ready and inspect have no game system behind them yet, so they're not here.
- **Screen (#28, #60, #176, #25).** `show_fps` / `show_ping` (top right, frames counted over 0.5 s; hidden while F3 is up), `captions` (each announcer line as text under the crosshair for 2 s, through one `Game::announce`), `hp_scaled` (the health bar against the most health seen this life, so 200-health customs and 50-health ones fill correctly; the client isn't told the mode's max, so its spawn health stands in), `ch_head_color` (a headshot's crosshair flash).
- **Controls (#18, #73, #5).** `grapple_toggle` (press to hook, press again to let go; reset on death), `scroll_wrap` / `scroll_skip` (a bit per weapon; number keys still select them), `left_handed` (both first-person models mirror sides and their tilt). Dash stays a tap (a toggle makes no sense for an impulse); CROUCH was already off every key page. A separate viewmodel FOV isn't done: the models are drawn in the world pass with the scene's projection, and their SIZE / DEPTH settings already do what a viewmodel FOV is used for.
- **Display (#218, #121, #112).** `vrr`: swap interval 1 and `maxFps()` capped at refresh - 3 (from the monitor's mode, or DEVICES' REFRESH RATE in fullscreen), the usual G-SYNC / FreeSync recipe that keeps the frame out of vsync's queue. `auto_iconify` (GLFW_AUTO_ICONIFY). `cpu_priority`: PRIORITIZE GAME can ask for HIGH_PRIORITY_CLASS instead of ABOVE_NORMAL (`setGamePriority(level)`).
- **Menus (#161, #165, #193, #77).** `remember_tab`; `confirm_leave` (QUIT, DISCONNECT, STOP HOSTING arm on the first click as "SURE? ..." and act on a second within 3 s); `auto_reconnect` (a "connection to server lost" drop goes back to the last address after 2 s, up to 3 tries, not for a kick, a refusal, a demo or your own server) and RECONNECT next to JOIN GAME; `queue_flash` (a filled queue also plays the finish chime and flashes the taskbar if the game isn't focused).
- **Editor (#195, #196).** Redo: each undo step now keeps its own op beside its inverse (`edUndoFwd_`), so undo moves the step to `edRedo_` and Ctrl+Y / Ctrl+Shift+Z does it again; a new edit clears the redo list; drags and coalesced nudges redo to where they ended. Autosave: every 60 s with unsaved changes the map goes to `editor-autosave.qmap` in the data folder (outside `maps/`, so it never shows in map lists); SAVE removes it; RESTORE AUTOSAVE appears in the panel when one is left from before.

## Settings: batch 2, the 3s (second half)

- **Settings search (#132).** `tools/settings_index.py` reads game.cpp's settings draw functions at build time (a CMake step, like the embedded maps) and writes `settings_index.h`: each widget label written as a string literal, and the tab / MORE page / keys sub-page it's on, so the index can't go stale when a setting moves. The SEARCH field (top right of the settings panel) matches labels and key action names. While the field has focus, up to 14 matches replace the page body (an overlay over the page would sit on top of live widgets); a click on one opens its page, and a click anywhere else closes the list and goes to the page. Only this field's focus counts (`Ui::focused(id)`), not `typing()`, which a slider or colour being typed into also sets.
- **Third bind slot (#140).** `kBindSlots = 3`. Binds are saved as three values per action; older games read the first two and ignore the third, so a config still works if you go back a version. Key rows are now three 82 px slots.
- **Console binds and aliases (#141, #142).** `bind <key> <command>` (`cbind_<code>` in the config), `unbind`, `binds`, `alias <name> <commands>` (`;`-separated, `alias_<name>`), `unalias`, `exec <file>` (from the data folder) and `toggle <cvar>`. A command bind runs on key press, after the action binds. `autoexec.cfg` runs once at start (not in the UI test, which must start from defaults).
- **All settings export / import (#167).** EXPORT puts the config as text (`quark-settings:` then `key=value` lines, keys included, your name / Discord / servers left out) on the clipboard; IMPORT applies every line it recognises and says how many. RESET ALL arms on the first click (SURE?) and keeps name, Discord name and server lists.
- **Demos (#143, #144, #145).** The DEMOS page sorts by date / name / size / map (`demo_sort`), shows sizes, and DELETE asks first. `auto_record`: OFF / EVERY MATCH / RANKED, started on entering the match. `demo_keep` (0 = all) deletes the oldest demos past N when an auto recording starts (never the one being written).
- **Sensitivity converter (#9).** Quark's yaw is 0.022 degrees per count x sensitivity, the same as Source and Quake, so CS2 / Quake / Apex / TF2 carry over 1:1; Valorant is 0.07 and Overwatch 2 / Call of Duty 0.0066. USE <value> sets yours.
- **Fullscreen resolution (#113).** `fs_width` / `fs_height` from `glfwGetVideoModes` (0 = the desktop mode). Applied when fullscreen is (re)entered.
- **Practice (#148).** Admin command `practice savepos|loadpos|god|passive|reset` on your own server, refused in ranked or when another human is connected (so it can't be used against anyone). loadpos sets position, velocity and view and sends EV_SPAWN so prediction snaps there instead of smoothing; passive strips the bots' fire buttons; god makes `canHurt` false for you.
- **Players menu (#156).** Pause > HOST > PLAYERS: everyone on your server with KICK (not yourself), plus ADD BOT, KICK BOTS and RESTART, through the existing admin commands.
- **Race ghost (#179).** Your position every 50 ms on race maps; a finished run faster than the saved one is written to `ghosts/<map>.txt` and drawn as a see-through player along the run, by the time since your start. Capped at 20 minutes of points. Client only.
- **Team panel (#17).** `team_panel`: teammates' names and health bars on the left edge in team modes, and a square per enemy, lit while they're alive (off by default).
- **Match summary (#180).** `match_summary`: your shots, hits, accuracy, damage dealt and best streak for the match under the end-of-match scoreboard, counted on the client.
- **Demo rename (#139).** RENAME beside DELETE turns the row into a field; Enter renames the file (letters, digits and - kept, the rest _), Esc or a click away leaves it. **Not in this batch** (still 3s): a turning 3D model preview (#128) and moving the objective / notice / scoreboard boxes (#21), which come with batch 3. Viewmodel FOV: see Controls above.

## Thin surfaces are wallbang (owner, 2026-10-30: "any 1/8m surface should have the wallbang property on it")

- `wallbangBrush(b)` (map.h): the wallbang material, or any shot-stopping brush whose thinnest side is 1/8 m or less (0.126 with a rounding margin), whatever its material: a Coat (K) layer, a thin panel, trim. `raycastBullet` (server hit checks and every client prediction of them) and the map's `hasWallbang` flag use it, so a map with only thin brushes still takes the wallbang path. Movement, sight, splash and grapple rays are unchanged: a thin wall still blocks players and explosions. A bullet passes one or more wallbang brushes and takes the weapon's wallbang damage once, as before. Test: `testWallbang` (a 1/8 m box passes, a 1/4 m one stops).

## Map picker (owner, 2026-10-30: "you shouldnt get to pick maps that are not under the playable gamemode for the map, also map previews should exist in the picker in game; list should be scrollable and 7 wide")

- **Only the mode's maps.** The host, vs bots and in-game pickers list a map only if its `modes` include the chosen mode (a tourney rotation list: tourney; ranked: maps ranked for that ladder). The "NOT FOR THIS MODE" tag, its legend and the ranked-but-greyed cards are gone with them. A console `map` command can still start any map in any mode.
- **Previews.** Each map gets a 192x108 picture from above, made on the CPU when its info is read (once per map file, cached with its title and modes; about a millisecond): each column the colour of its highest surface by material and the map's palette, lit by height and a light from the top left, darker where the height steps. An indoor map's roof and any slab over most of the map are left out so the rooms show; clip and far-scenery brushes too. All previews go to the GPU as one atlas, only when the map list or a file changes, and draw through the swatch image program over the 2D batch (`Renderer::preview`; the swatch code now shares `drawImageQuads`). Why not a 3D render: the renderer holds one world (the menu backdrop or the match), so rendering 25 other maps would mean loading each one.
- **7 wide, scrolling.** Cards are 122 px wide (picture over the name, the mode it's for under it); as many rows as fit the band, the wheel scrolls the rest, and it opens scrolled to the current map. Clicking the picture picks the map too.

## Settings: batch 3, the 2s (video, visuals, audio, performance, latency, network, crosshair, accessibility)

Everything lives on VIDEO + AUDIO > MORE 2 (added to), MORE 4, MORE 5 and MORE 6 (new pages), the main VIDEO + AUDIO page and CROSSHAIR. Every renderer switch is a shader uniform that is 0 at its default (so the swatch program, which never gets them, draws as before) and is only set when it changes (`Renderer::setLook` compares); `Game::applyLive` pushes the batch into the renderer, mixer and connection once a frame as compares and atomic stores. Nothing costs anything per frame at its default.

- **Tone curve (#44).** `tone_curve`: off swaps the ACES-style filmic curve for a straight clamp (world, sky). The dynamic shader never had the curve, so players are unchanged.
- **Shadows (#45, #213).** `shadow_filter`: HARD (1 tap), SOFT (the 4 taps as before), SOFTER (16 taps, a texel apart: inside the caster rect's 2 texel margin, so no rect change). `shadow_dist`: the shadow map's half-width, 16 / 32 (as before) / 64 m: wider reaches farther but each texel covers more (blurrier). `shadow_world` / `shadow_players`: where dynamic shadows land (the world's / dynamic shader's `uShadowOn`); with neither on, the shadow pass isn't drawn at all. `own_shadow` off skips your first-person body entirely (it is drawn only as a shadow caster), so no shadow-pass geometry either.
- **Clean surfaces (#46).** `clean_surfaces` drops the palette materials' 2 m panel seams and per-panel shade noise (materials below 12). The newer textured materials are their look, so they stay.
- **Lighting (#47).** `flat_light`: the world shader ignores the lightmap (full sun and sky everywhere, as the see-through pass already does). The bake resolution and bounce count were **not** made settings: a bake is keyed and cached by map content and the official maps ship baked; another quality would bake every map again (minutes each) and need its own cache key. q's call.
- **Sky and far scenery (#48).** `simple_sky`: the sky shader returns the flat sky colour (no sun glare, no stars). `far_scenery` off skips the far-scenery chunks (they were already their own chunks; `WorldChunk::far` marks them).
- **Draw distance (#209).** `draw_dist` 150 / 250 / 400 m is the projection's far plane (was a fixed 400). The frustum's far plane already culls world chunks and dynamic objects, so a shorter distance also draws less.
- **Player brightness (#50) and always lit (#210).** `player_emit`: the dynamic shader's own-light share (0.3 as before; 1 = full colour in any shade). `players_lit`: players get sun visibility 1 and the per-player sun ray (the costliest thing per player) is skipped.
- **MSAA 8x (#53).** The cycle offers 8x; `msaa=8` loads. The renderer already clamped to `GL_MAX_SAMPLES`.
- **Show when not supported (#219).** MSAA levels above the GPU's maximum are labelled N/A with the maximum; 10-BIT OUTPUT says what the window got; BYPASS COMPOSITOR says Linux only elsewhere; the latent sync settings say Windows only, or to pick latent sync first; AUDIO LATENCY says LOW = NORMAL on Windows.
- **FOV mode (#74).** `fov_mode`: FOV read as horizontal on this screen (as before), vertical, or horizontal at 4:3 (Hor+). `Game::hfov()` turns it into the horizontal degrees every camera / zoom / sensitivity use takes (at most 170).
- **Outlines (#86, #211, #212).** A ninth outline preset, CUSTOM, takes `outline_friendly_color` / `outline_enemy_color` (MORE 4's pickers; the PLAYER tab's cycles list it automatically through `kOutlineColors`). `outline_alpha` mixes the outline toward the body colour (a uniform in the dynamic shader). `outline_team_width` / `outline_enemy_width` (-1 = PLAYER OUTLINE WIDTH). glTF player models have no edge data, so a real outline would need an inverted-hull pass per model (a cost): a hint says so instead.
- **Fixed team colours (#87).** `fixed_teams` with `team_a_color` / `team_b_color`: while spectating or playing a demo, team modes draw team A and B in these instead of "yours / the enemy" (which made team A always your friendly colour).
- **Tracers (#101), rope (#103), trails (#215).** `tracer_mode` classic pale yellow / the shooter's team / `tracer_color` (each tracer carries its colour, so no lookups when drawn). `rope_color` (was a fixed near-black). `trails` off / short (40%) / full and `trail_opacity` for the rocket, bolt, knife, syringe and orb streaks.
- **Colour presets (#111).** COPY / PASTE COLORS: every colour setting (team, outlines, crosshair, tracer, rope, fullbright, name) as one `quark-colors:` line, only those keys read back. Three slots (`color_preset_1..3`) SAVE and LOAD it.
- **Deband and 10-bit (#204).** `dither`: plus or minus half an 8-bit step of interleaved gradient noise on the world, players and sky (no bands in sky and fog). `out10`: asks GLFW for a 10-bit window at the next start (falls back to 8 if none); when granted, the scene targets (MSAA / render scale) are RGB10_A2 too so the bits reach the window.
- **Accessibility (#58, #59, #61, #108).** `it_palette`: tag's "it" in your enemy colour and the rest in your team's (was fixed red / blue), and the IT banner too. `split_palette` / `split_words`: race splits in your team / enemy colours, and AHEAD / BEHIND after the time. `reduce_flash`: no muzzle flashes, the bomb and stuck stickies stop blinking, explosions at 40% opacity, the damage flash at most 0.3. `safe_colors`: the OK / error colours (`kGreenOk` / `kRed`, no longer const) become blue and orange, told apart under every common colour blindness.
- **Audio output (#34, #140, #231, #232).** `channels` stereo / mono / swapped, mixed after the voices. `stereo_width` (the pan factor, 0.8 before), `behind_muffle` (scales the X8 behind-you dip and low-pass), `hear_range` (the 90 m cut-off, 30 - 150 m). `occlusion`: one map ray from your ears as each 3D sound starts; a wall between makes it about 7 dB quieter and low-passed (~1 kHz); off by default because it changes what you hear. `dyn_range`: CLEAN (the soft limiter at 0.08 instead of 0.3), NORMAL (as before), NIGHT (a 5 ms / 300 ms level follower pulling loud passages down about 4:1 over -20 dB, then +6 dB). HRTF was **not** done: it needs an HRTF data set and a convolution per voice (a real mixer cost and an asset choice). Sound when unfocused: `bg_volume`, next to the existing MUTE IN BACKGROUND.
- **Volume (#234).** VOLUME goes to 150% (`volume` up to 1.5, shown in %); `vol_db` shows every volume as its gain in dB; `vol_perceptual` makes the gain the slider's square.
- **Audio latency (#116).** `audio_latency`: ALSA 10 / 20 (as before) / 40 ms; CoreAudio 2 / 3 / 6 buffers; WASAPI SAFE keeps twice the queue (LOW is NORMAL there: the owner found anything under one engine period worse). The output is reopened when it changes.
- **Sound pack, callouts and cues (#63, #65, #154, #172, #173).** `force_pack`: everyone's sounds in your pack (`Game::soundPackOf`). `streak_calls` all / mine / off. `time_warn` off / 60 + 30 (as before) / also a count of the last 10 s (ticks, then THREE TWO ONE), `time_warn_text` as notices too. `cue_mute` / `ann_mute`: a bit per sound id; `Audio::setMuted` drops them at `play` / `play3D` with one bit test; muted announcer lines also skip their caption.
- **Visualize sounds (#228).** `sound_viz`: the mixer keeps the 3D sounds started (game thread, only while on) and they're drawn as STEPS / SHOT / BOOM / HOOK / HURT / DASH round the crosshair toward where they came from, fading over 0.8 s.
- **Net graph and packet loss protection (#17, #115).** `net_graph`: ping and loss over 12 s, snapshots in, input packets out and how many commands each carries, and the interpolation delay, at the right edge (F3 unchanged). The command rate itself stays one packet per tick (fewer would add latency). `cmd_redundancy` 2 / 4 (as before) / 8 commands per input packet (`NetClient::setCmdsPerPacket`; the server already reads up to 16).
- **Downloaded maps (#194).** `map_downloads` off: a server's map you don't have ends the connection with a message instead of downloading. `map_cache_max` keeps the newest N in maps/downloads (pruned after each download and when lowered); the page shows the count and size, CLEAR DOWNLOADED MAPS empties it.
- **Light cache (#117).** `light_cache_max` (was a fixed 32 bakes), `bake_threads` (0 = all but one core, as before), the cache's bakes and size, CLEAR LIGHT BAKES (shipped bakes stay). A prebake of every map was **not** added: the official maps ship baked, and baking your own maps at once would hold every core for minutes.
- **Latent sync (#114).** `latent_safety_us`: the spare time before the blank (the fixed 100 us before). `latent_slow`: a frame that misses its blank flips now (a tear, as before) or WAITs for the next one (no tear, a refresh later). Windows only, like latent sync.
- **Bypass compositor (#223).** `bypass_compositor`: Linux on X11, `_NET_WM_BYPASS_COMPOSITOR` on the window: GLFW's (fullscreen only), ALWAYS (a window unredirected too) or NEVER. The X11 calls are declared by hand so Xlib's macros stay out of game.cpp.
- **Menu cursor (#230).** `cursor_size` system / x1 / x1.5 / x2 and `cursor_color` white / black / yellow / team: an arrow made with `glfwCreateCursor` (again only when they change).
- **USE toggle (#149).** `use_toggle`: a press holds USE (plant / defuse) until the next press, like GRAPPLE TOGGLE; dying lets go. On VIDEO + AUDIO > MORE 6 (CONTROLS is another branch's this batch).
- **Crosshair (#145, #217).** `ch_spread`: a ring of dots at the held weapon's spread (its tangent over the view's), shown when it's wider than the crosshair. `ch_cooldown`: 32 dots round the crosshair that fill as the refire runs out (hidden when ready). `ch_pixels`: the crosshair's sizes as exact pixels, not scaled from 720p. All three ride in the crosshair code.

## Settings: batch 3, the 2s (HUD, social, controls, menus, host, editor, launcher)

Worker 2's half of the 2-scored items. Batch-3 keys are table-driven (`b3Keys` in config.cpp: type, bounds, default), parsed by `setBatch3` before the old keys and written by `batch3Text`, so each new key is one line and always clamped. Three MORE pages are new (GAMEPLAY > MORE 2 / 3 / 4, CONTROLS > MORE 2, PLAYER > MORE), each with its screen path for the UI test and its page function in `tools/settings_index.py` so the settings search finds every label. Nothing new runs per frame at the defaults: every feature sits behind its setting's check (a compare), and the three that track state from frame to frame (health, alerts, auto-hide) are a few compares each.

**Feedback (GAMEPLAY > MORE 2)**
- **Damage numbers (#16).** `dmg_num_color` (a normal hit; headshots stay red and big hits gold so the meaning stays), `dmg_num_stack` (quick hits add up / a number a hit / one running total a target until it fades) and `dmg_num_time` (0.3 to 3 s, 0.9 as before).

## Movement: wall teleporters keep speed, clip refuses the grapple (owner, 2026-10-30: "clip should also refuse hook"; the teleporter bug from a map review)
- **Wall teleporters kept only the clipped speed.** A teleporter block is solid, and the touch check (pmove.cpp) ran after the slide move had already clipped the velocity against it. So whenever a step ended inside the wall rather than just short of it, `n * length(s.vel)` used what was left after the clip. At 60 m/s into a wall teleporter, 4 starts out of 8 came out at under 1 m/s. Now the exit speed is the larger of the speeds before and after the slide. Floor teleporters behaved the same before; it only showed on walls, because the speed was horizontal. Test: movement, 8 start offsets at 60 m/s, all keep 60.
- **Clip refuses the grapple.** Clip (material 9) let the hook pass through it to whatever was behind, which made the invisible lids and the clip over walls hook points nobody could see. `Map::raycast` takes `hitClip` (only the grapple and the bots' hook planning pass it). A hook that reaches clip hits it and lets go, like nograpple. Shots and sight still pass clip. Cost: none on the shot path (the extra test only runs on brushes that don't stop shots).

## Servers: the OFFICIAL tag (owner, 2026-10-30: official servers get a tag in the server browser that can't be faked by naming a server "official")

- **What.** JOIN's server list draws an OFFICIAL chip (the ranked gold: gold Rubik text at hint size on a dark gold chip with a gold border; dark text was tried and the text outline swallowed it) before the name of an official server and sorts the official servers first, ahead of favourites and the chosen sort. The official servers are now one box, 75.127.7.139: production on UDP 27500, testing on 27501.
- **How it can't be faked.** The client has the list built in (`kOfficialServers`, `src/game/master.cpp`; `isOfficialServer(ip, port)` / `isOfficialServer("a.b.c.d:port")`, `master.h`) and matches it against the address the info reply actually came from (`ServerInfo::official`, set once per reply in `ServerBrowser::poll`, and for a silent internet entry from its listed address), never against the name the server reports. A spoofed UDP source would also need the reply to reach the browser's own socket for a query it sent, and joining goes to that same address. The string form is strict (four dotted parts, no leading zeros, a port required), so lookalikes such as `75.127.7.139:27502`, `75.127.7.13:27500` or `075.127.7.139:27500` don't match (`testOfficialServers`, group ranked).
- **Why built in, not from the Worker.** `/list`'s format stays one `ip:port` per line (old clients take any short line as an address), and a flag served by the Worker would only be as trustworthy as the Worker's reply; a list in the signed game can only change with a signed release. The cost: if the official servers move, `kOfficialServers` must be updated and a release shipped (docs/HANDOVER.md says so).
- **Cost.** One bool per server, computed when its reply arrives; the draw adds a static string, a measured width, one rounded rect and one text per official row: no allocation a frame.

## Modes: power (MODE_POWER 14; owner, 2026-10-30: "each team has player count * 2500 health, nobody can die, just soak damage; when total team health reaches 0 they lose")

- **Rules.** Team vs with one health pool per team: `poolPerPlayer` (2500, a new param of the new `pool` object) x the players on it, full at the start of each round. In `Server::damagePlayer`, an enemy's hit (after the ruleset's multipliers, so instagib takes 101) comes off the victim's team pool while the round is live, and never off the player's own health: nobody dies of damage, and knockback lands as before. ## Map checks: the walk grid walks ramps (engine work list, 2026-10-30: "the walk check can't see ramps")

- **What was wrong.** `tests/walkgrid.h` (the collision-checked walk behind the spawn / route checks, `quark_tests maps`, `bounds` and `quark_maptool`'s check / route / metrics) put every solid brush into its columns as its whole box, `box.mn.y .. box.mx.y`. A wedge, corner, tetra or cut slope was a block as tall as its high end, so a 15 m decline was a 15 m wall. Mine's haul road (a 25 degree wedge, 15 m over 31 m) was found only by an 18.6 s detour, and Mine's lava ramp and QK7's helix (a solid wedge, then cut ramps every 5 m) weren't found at all.
- **Now.** A slope you can walk (`WalkGrid::walkableSlope`: standing on the floor, `up` 0, or a cut box, and no steeper than pmove's `floorFace` limit, n.y >= 0.7071 - 1e-4, tan 1.0002) goes into each column as what of it is over that column's hull footprint (`columnSpan`): a wedge / corner / tetra up to `brushTopOver` of the footprint (where pmove's box collision rests the feet: the slope's highest point under the hull); a cut box between its planes' bounds over the footprint's corners (the top no higher than each up-facing plane's highest corner, the bottom no lower than each down-facing plane's lowest; a side plane that cuts the whole footprint away leaves the column out). That is exact on a ramp's face and errs high (more solid, never a false way through) only where two planes meet over the column. A 45 degree ramp rises 0.25 m per 0.25 m column, well inside the 0.55 m step. Steeper slopes, and slopes on their side or hung from a ceiling, stay whole boxes: stepping up a 63 degree face column by column would climb walls pmove slides you off.
- **Standing on a ramp.** The feet rest on the slope's highest point under the hull, at its edge, so the 10 cm support check (floor within 0.3 m of the centre, 2 cm under the feet) failed on a slope. Each merged span now carries a slack, (0.4 - 0.3) x the steepness + 5 mm, and the support probe goes that much deeper there (0 on boxes: flat floors check exactly as before).
- **Results.** Routes (`quark_maptool route`, before -> after): Mine haul road foot to top 18.6 s (round the long way) -> 3.7 s; Mine lava ramp unreachable -> 3.3 s; QK7 ground to the first helix landing (5 m) unreachable -> 7.0 s, to the top (30 m) unreachable -> 21.1 s. Standing spots: Mine 535,394 -> 543,740, QK7 264,086 -> 303,058, Datacenter 1,609,545 -> 1,626,575. The shipped maps are unchanged except Cobblestone (151,829 -> 152,465; its slopes), and all still pass.
- **Speed.** Only slope brushes take the new path (boxes are rasterised as before). Raster lap on Datacenter (166 cut boxes) about 120 -> 165 ms and Mine 100 -> 133 ms (best of 4, threaded, a shared CPU); the whole build is unchanged within noise (Datacenter 956 -> 932 ms for the header's single-threaded build: fewer spots fail the off-centre search). A first version clipped each cut's faces per column (`cutBounds`) and took the raster to 670 ms; the corner bounds give the same spots.
- **The maptool copy.** `tools/maptool/map/walkfast.cpp` (the threaded copy of the build) uses the same `walkableSlope` / `columnSpan` / `mergeSpan` / `RawSpan` from the header; `walkverify` still finds the two builds identical on Mine, QK7 and Datacenter.
- **Test.** `testWalkGrid` (maps group): a 4 m deck with no way up is unreachable; a 27 degree wedge, a 45 degree wedge, a cut ramp (y <= x over its box), a wedge rising along z and a corner ramp each make it reachable (and the way down walks); a 63 degree wedge and an upside-down wedge don't.

## Map format 12: walk teleporters (owner, 2026-10-30: "teleporters should have toggle for carry all speed or max at walking velocity, in solar they should all be walking speed")

- **What.** A teleporter has a speed mode: CARRY (as before, the default) or WALK. ## Bots: the tick and round spawns don't depend on slot order (map worker, 2026-10-30: "the team listed second scores about 3% more, on any map": Mine koth, 48 seeds)

- **What depended on order.** (1) In `Server::simulate()` each bot thought and then ran its command in slot order, so bot 5 decided on a world bots 0-4 had already moved, shot in and killed in this tick (positions, `lastFire`, deaths), and the per-tick route budget (`kPathsPerTick` 2) went to the low slots first. A team added first takes the low slots. (2) `startRound` spawned players in slot order and `spawnPlayer` scores spawns by distance from and sight of alive enemies: the second team dodged the first team's fresh spawns, and last round's survivors still counted as alive where they stood.
- **Now.** (1) Every bot thinks first, all on the tick-start world, then everyone's commands run, starting from a different connected player each tick (`tick % players`), so no slot is always first. One extra pass over at most 32 slots and a 32-entry command array: no measurable cost. Humans' commands run in the same rotated order. (2) `startRound` marks everyone dead before the spawns and, in team modes, `spawnPlayer` ignores enemies spawned in the same batch, so each team's spawn choice is the same whichever went first. Respawns during play are unchanged.
- **Numbers.** The 3% didn't reproduce as a significant effect. Share of the team in the high slots: `quark_maptool bots` Summit koth 180 s, 200 runs: score 0.503 +- 0.010, kills 0.502 +- 0.003; Bastion vs 120 s, 400 runs: score 0.492 +- 0.012; Mine koth 300 s, the 48 seeds both ways: score 0.506 +- 0.020 (pairwise +0.011 +- 0.044). So 3% on 48 seeds is within noise. In-process, Bastion vs 4v4 60 s, 600 seeds each way (1200 runs): before, kills 0.4925 +- 0.0050 (1.5 sigma), score 0.490 +- 0.010; with the tick fix alone 0.4920 +- 0.0051; with both fixes kills 0.5053 +- 0.0050, score 0.506 +- 0.010. Both are within noise; the fixes take away the mechanisms, so nothing depends on order by construction.
- **Test.** `quark_tests tickorder`: a hook hashes every player's position, velocity, health and alive at each bot think and checks every think in a tick sees the same world, no bot thinks twice in a tick and all 8 bots are first at some point; then Bastion vs 4v4, 40 seeds each way, the team-A score share with A listed first vs second within 4 standard errors. `quark_tests orderbias [seeds] [map] [mode] [seconds] [perTeam] [firstSeed]` measures it at scale (`QUARK_LEGACY_ORDER=1` for the old tick order).
- **Seeded tests.** The rotated order changes every seeded bot match. `testBots`'s FFA-vs-experts run moved from seed 0x5EED0001 (the standing player now goes 40 s unhit, as 2 of 20 seeds did before too) to 0x5EED0003; over 20 seeds the old and new orders give the same spread (the player's deaths 34 vs 32 in total, kills 3 to 9, grapples 4 to 16).

## Bomb: spawns by role, not by team (q, 2026-10-30: the recommended option, confirmed)

- **Why.** `modeSpawnType` started team A on the map's `a` spawns and team B on `b` in every round. q's timing rule for bomb maps (the attackers reach a site in about 5 s, the defenders 1 s later) is built for the side that attacks, and team A attacks the first half (bomb.qlogic: `attackers = half > 0 and roundNo > half ? B : A`). So after halftime team B attacked from the defence's end and team A defended from the attack's: one team had the short way in both halves. On War (115 m vs 144 m) team A won 7 of 8 matches.
- **Now.** In bomb, the attackers start on `a` and the defenders on `b` (or the map's `bomb-a` / `bomb-b` set, format 11), whichever team that is: `team == attackers ? a : b`. So `a` means "the attack's spawns" on a bomb map, as the maps were built (team A attacks first). `roundSetup` sets the attackers before the round's spawns, so every round, halftime included, spawns on the new sides; respawns in the warmup use the first half's attackers (team A). Custom modes based on bomb get the same through `mapSpawn`. Other modes are unchanged.
- **Checks.** The walk check already asks both sides' spawns to reach every site, and the sight check pairs `a` with `b`, so nothing changes there: `quark_maptool check` passes all 15 checks on Depot, Datacenter and War (maps-1196). Quarry isn't in any worktree here.
- **Test.** `testBombSpawnRoles` (group `rules spawns`): `modeSpawnType` for each team in each half on Depot and on a map with a `bomb-a` / `bomb-b` set, vs still by team; then a real Depot match with halftime after round 1: in round 1's freeze team A stands on an `a` spawn and team B on a `b` one, in round 2's team B on `a` and team A on `b`.

## Glass: sloped panes are their shape, not their box (a map review, 2026-10-30: Museum's glass dome on maps-1196, four 40 x 6 m slabs floating over the roof)

- **What was a box.** Only the drawing and a few point tests. The game drew every pane with `translucentBox(brush.box)` (plain and breakable glass, and the editor's baked view), and the see-through looks and breakable blocks with `boxFaces` (the box's 6 faces), so a pane on a wedge, corner, tetra or cut drew as its bounding box: a dome face's box is mostly air. `Map::glassAt` (which pane a shot or bolt is in), `blockAt` and `crumbleAt` tested the box grown by 3 cm: where sloped panes' boxes overlap (a dome's four faces all do), a shot on one face could break another. Shards started anywhere in the pane's box. Collision (`sweepAxis` with `brushObstacle`), every ray (`rayBrush`: shots, sight, the grapple) and the dash-through test already used the real shape.
- **Now.** `Renderer::translucentBrush` draws a slope or cut as its faces (`slopeTriangles`, map.h: a fan over `slopePolygons`), a box as before; `boxFaces` gives see-through looks and breakable blocks on a slope their faces too, with the outline distances `Renderer::slope` uses. `brushNear(b, p, eps)` (map.h: the box and each sloped plane, grown by eps) replaces the box tests in `glassAt`, `blockAt` and `crumbleAt`. `glassShards` takes the pane's brush: on a slope a shard's start is drawn again (up to 12 times) until it is in the shape; a box draws exactly the same numbers as before, and the server and the clients (`GlassFx.brush`, new) pass the same brush, so they still make the same shards. A shattered sloped pane's glitter starts at its pieces' starts when it would be off the shape. No cost for box panes; a slope's triangles are made per frame like the box's quads (no shipped map has a sloped pane yet).
- **Test.** `quark_tests slopedglass`: a breakable wedge, a second one rising the other way in the same box, and plain glass on a cut box of the same slope. Both draw 8 triangles, 157.7 m2, every corner on the shape (the box: 208 m2); the cut is air over its low end and solid under it; a shot down at x 7 hits it at 0.50 m (the box's top: 4); a player box drops onto the slope; `glassAt` picks the wedge whose shape holds the point (the box test picked the first); a shatter's 600 shards are all in the wedge (290 off it with only the box), and a box pane's shards are unchanged; breaking one wedge leaves the other standing. Checked by eye on Museum (maps-1196): the dome draws as four sloped glass faces with the rafters under them.

## Rockets: splash did no damage on servers (q, 2026-10-30: "explosions aren't registering, they do no damage on my Oracle box server"; direct hits worked, splash did nothing, no hit markers)

- **Cause.** Since 1.19.3 the shooter's game reports its own rocket hits (`Game::reportImpact` -> `reportBlast`, `hit` commands) and `Server::explode` leaves every victim but the shooter to that report. The game reported the blast at the exact point its rocket's path met the floor or wall (`q.pos + d / len * hit` for the predicted copy, `pr.pos + d / len * hit` for the server's copy drawn ahead). `reportBlast`'s cover test then cast its rays from that point, on or (by rounding) a hair inside the surface, and `Map::raycast` hits a box at t = 0 from a point on its face, so the centre ray and both F16 rays were "blocked" and every splash victim was skipped: the report went out with no victims. The server's own blast never had this: `stepProjectile` sets it off 5 cm short of the surface. A direct hit's point is on the player's box, not the map, so it still counted.
- **Not ping.** Reproduced on a local dedicated server (1v1, 250 Hz, a UDP delay proxy at 20, 80 and ~220 ms round trip, with jitter and loss) with two real games under Xvfb, the shooter aiming at the floor 1.2 m beside a standing player: 1.19.3 counted 4 of 20 blasts the server would have hit (the 4 came from the "vanished projectile" path, which reports from a point in the air), the fix 14 of 14. Any reported blast on a surface was affected, at any ping.
- **Fix.** `blastPoint` (pmove.h, `kBlastBackoff` 0.05 m) is where a projectile goes off: 5 cm back along its flight from where it met the surface or player. `stepProjectile` uses it (unchanged: it was `max(0, best - 0.05)`), and `reportImpact` backs every reported rocket blast off the same way. The cover test is one shared function, `blastReaches` (pmove.h), used by both `Server::explode` and `Game::reportBlast`, so the two can't drift apart again. Where the explosion is drawn (`localBoom`) is unchanged. No wire change.
- **Test.** `testClientHits` (group materials): a floor blast 0.8 m from a player reaches them from `blastPoint`, not from the surface point (exactly on it or 1e-5 inside), and a player under the floor stays behind cover.

## Clean surfaces on every look (owner, 2026-10-31: "clean surfaces should apply to the custom surfaces too not just default ones")
- VIDEO > CLEAN SURFACES only took the panel seams and shade noise off the palette materials (0-11). Now every look from 12 on (ice, sand, wood, brick, the 400+ surface looks, the large patterns as their base, the see-through and feature blocks) draws as its average, flat: its albedo on tops and on sides separately (grass: green tops, earth sides), its see-through alpha and its glow. - How: when it's on, `Renderer::buildCleanMeans` draws each look the map uses once, with the map's own world program and this frame's palette, as a 4 m square (64 px) on a top and on a side. That's cheaper per pixel with it on, and unchanged with it off (one uniform branch). It's redone only when the world program, the palette or high contrast change: a few dozen 64 px draws and one readback while a map loads.
