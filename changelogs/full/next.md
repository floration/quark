## Netcode: client-side feedback at high ping
- Owner, 2026-11-04: "the game still behaves strangely at high ping, like on screen damage waiting for server, same with rocket projectile exploding it will just sit on a wall not exploding". The netcode rule (everything client-side except deaths) covered the damage itself (`sendHits`, PR #103) but not its feedback.
- **Hit feedback:** the flash, hitsound and damage number came only from the server's EV_HIT, a round trip after the client had already worked the hit out. `Game::hitFeedback` now runs from `sendHits` for every reported hit. What was shown is kept per victim (`shownDmg_`), and an EV_HIT for that victim is matched against it: the part already shown is dropped, anything past it (damage the server adds on its own: stickies, bubbles, ricochets, a weapon set's damage rules) is shown then. Unmatched shown damage expires after max(1 s, 2 x ping + 0.5 s), so a hit the server refused doesn't eat a later one. MATCH SUMMARY's hits and damage still count the server's numbers. Demos (no hits are reported in playback) show the recorded EV_HITs as before.
- **Projectile copies at high ping:** your predicted rocket / bolt / knife was dropped if the server's copy hadn't come after 0.6 s, and the server's copy was drawn at most 0.4 s ahead of its snapshot position. At a ping near or over those, the copy vanished or the server's rocket was drawn from up to a round trip behind and only blew up when the server's explosion arrived, so it sat on the wall. Both now scale with your ping: the copy lives max(0.6, 1.5 x ping + 0.3) s (at most 3 s), the lead is up to max(0.4, 1.5 x ping + 0.1) s (at most 2.5 s).
- **Stickies, thrown knives and syringes** (owner, 2026-11-04: "all should be in line locally like the rocket is"): a predicted copy now flies from the click for a sticky and a thrown syringe too (the server's throw speeds, `weaponAlt()`, also for the knife, which used the constant). A sticky copy that lands on a wall stays drawn there until the server's copy takes over; one on a player or a syringe is hidden at the hit, as before for the knife. Hits: a thrown knife in a player (its copy, the server's copy's lead, or a vanished one) is reported by the game (`reportImpact` WPN_KNIFE: knife damage with falloff, 3x the knife's knock, no headshot, as `stepProjectile`); your sticky detonate reports its blast at once (`reportBlast` WPN_STICKY). The server skips its own hits for those (`reportsHits`) only for a game that said `clienthits 2`, and for stickies only the ones their owner set off (`ownerDetonate_`): one set off by another blast (a chain) stays the server's, since the game never saw it go, and a sticky that runs out of time vanishes without a blast. The client's detonate counts a sticky as armed half a round trip sooner (it first sees the server's copy that long after launch), so it sets off the same ones the server does. Syringe heals stay the server's (a heal isn't damage feedback; EV_HEALED). **Protocol 70**: a 69 server would take the new reports on top of its own hits (double damage).

## Latency / F3
- **Frame pacing graph** (owner, 2026-11-04: "the f3 screen should also have a frametime graph on it to check frame pacing"; "5 second history ... 50% of your frametime up and down, unless somehow you escape it then that becomes the new max/min while its in the graph").   - Width: up to 40% of the screen, so it stays left of the HUD's centre bars.

## Menus: main menu icons drawn as shapes
- **Why:** q, 2026-11-04: "icons are too pixely, should be drawn with shapes instead of images". The strip's eight icons (PLAY, PRACTICE, CREATE, RANKS, SETTINGS, DISCORD, INFO, QUIT) were built from thick 2D quads (one per line segment, arcs as 10-18 short segments) whose corners were snapped to whole pixels, with no anti-aliasing: diagonals and arcs came out stair-stepped and the stroke width wobbled.
- **What:** new `Renderer::icon(id, cx, cy, size, colour)`: one quad per icon (it was up to 30 quads), its shape a signed distance on the old 24 x 24 grid evaluated in the UI fragment shader (round-capped lines, arcs, rounded boxes, a polar-repeated gear), coverage over one screen pixel. Smooth at any size and scale; the shapes keep the old designs (the gear now a true outline with 8 teeth, the grid 3 x 2 cells, lines with round ends). The `UiIcon` ids live in renderer.h; `seg2D` / `arc2D` / `mainIcon` (game.cpp) are gone.
- **Supersampled (q, 2026-11-04: "the main menu icons and text should always render at 400% scale so its always super smooth"):** the icons, and all text drawn by the main menu's screen (`Renderer::uiSupersample` on its quads: a +4 flag in the vertex's split-Q byte), take 4 x 4 samples a pixel in the UI shader, each edge smoothed over a quarter pixel: the same result as drawing them at 400% and scaling down, without a 400% framebuffer (10240 x 5760 at 1440p). Text elsewhere (HUD, other menus) is unchanged.
- **QUARK on the main screen (q: "it doesnt say quark anywhere on the main screen"):** the full wordmark (the baked logo, team colours) 40 design px high in the top bar, centred between the page and the name chip's widest; left out when that gap is narrower than the logo or the panels are stacked. The baked logo is supersampled on this screen too (it was drawn 2.5x smaller than baked with one bilinear tap). `Renderer::logoWidth` added.
- **The strip's Q (q: "weirdly slightly off"):** it sat 12 design px above the middle of the strip's head (ink centre at 32 of 0-88); now its ink centre is at 44, and it moves 0.75 px right so the bowl, not bowl plus tail, is on the strip's centre line.
- **The logo, livelier (q: "the quark logo looks kind of bland"; q picked "glossy arcade", option D of six mockups):** `tools/make_logo.py` bakes a drop shadow (the outlined word blurred, radius 6 px at the 2160p bake, moved 5 px right and 9 down) into the texture's B channel, which was a copy of A (it can also slant the word: ITALIC, 0 for now). The UI shader adds the gloss: the letters' top half lit (x1.4 at the top to x1.0 at the middle), a step to the bottom half's x0.8-0.65, smoothed over one screen pixel (uLogoInk: the ink rows the bake writes), and the shadow under the outline at 75%. logoQ crops the Q's own outline columns (kLogoQX0/1, no longer a hard-coded 134) and leaves the shadow out (UARK's would show at the crop's edge). logo() centres the word's ink (kLogoInkX0/1). Same draw: one quad, no extra pass. The wordmark on the main screen is centred on the screen where it fits.
- **Cost:** fewer vertices than before (8 quads for the whole strip instead of ~120); the shader work runs only on the icons' own pixels (about 44 x 44 each at 1440p, 16 shape evaluations each) and the main menu's text pixels (16 atlas reads each instead of 1); nothing changes in a match.

## Display: exclusive fullscreen at a custom size (owner, 2026-11-05)
- applyDisplayMode resized the renderer to at least the desktop's size in every fullscreen mode. In exclusive fullscreen with VIDEO > FULLSCREEN SIZE smaller than the desktop, GLFW's resize callback (fired inside glfwSetWindowMonitor) was overwritten with the desktop size, so the game drew a desktop-sized picture into the smaller screen: anchored bottom-left and cut off. - RANKED (main menu) is a queue: picking a ladder joins the lowest-ping ranked server on that ladder with 1+ players and a free slot, from the list the main menu refreshes every 10 s; with none, it hosts on the ladder's own map (greenhouse / dojo / spire), always on the public list. ## Weapon models: animated parts
- Owner, 2026-11-05: "each weapon should have animations like the sticky cycles the drum, the shotgun racks, whatever". Weapon overrides (glTF) may now name moving parts `anim_<part>` with the part's pivot as the node's translation (docs/ASSETS.md "Animated parts"; the model files come separately).
- **Loader** (`loadGltf`, gltf.cpp): a node named `anim_<part>` (lower-cased, Blender's `.001` stripped) starts a part: its pivot is its translation through its parents, its own rotation / scale are ignored, and its primitives (and its children's) are tagged with it (`MeshPrim::anim`, `MeshData::anims`). The triangles stay in model space, assembled, so the export, bounds and editors see the model whole. At most 12 parts a model (`kMaxAnimParts`); more stay in the body. A file with no anim nodes loads exactly as before (tested).
- **ModelMesh::parts** (models.h): `meshToModel` splits each part into its own mesh with triangles relative to the pivot, plus its name and pivot. Generic for every model kind (player models can use the same for their joints later): `Renderer::mesh` / `viewMesh` / `viewMeshXf` draw every part still at its pivot, so a draw path that doesn't animate them draws the model whole as before.
- **Motions** (`weaponPartMotion`, models.cpp; pure, tested): per weapon and part, from a `WeaponAnimState` (time since your last shot or alt shot with the weapon, its refire, the shot count, the laser's spin, a clock, the antenna's spring). All timings are fractions of the shot's own refire (`shotRefire`, so a scoped rifle or the bubble stream fits its cadence) and finish before the next shot: rifle bolt 2.5 cm back in 55 ms; rocket round hidden to 30% of the refire, back by 90%; sniper bolt up (-70 deg about Z: the .50's handle is on the left wall) / back 1.5 cm (its slot) / forward / down across 12-80%; laser coil up to 3 turns/s, spinning up over 0.3 s and down over 0.6 s; shotgun pump 6 cm across 10-70%; sticky drum 60 deg a shot over 40-200 ms, the angle adding up (shots x 60, mod 360: the top chamber is empty at rest, so turning back would flicker it); crossbow string snaps 1 cm forward in 30 ms (further and its ends pass the cams) and is drawn back by 60%, the loaded bolt slides in by 90%; bubble lever -25 deg and back in 160 ms; syringe plunger 3 cm in 60 ms, back over the cooldown; locator sweep 1 turn / 2 s, antenna a 2.8 Hz damped spring kicked by your turning. The table is in docs/ASSETS.md.
- **Driven by prediction:** `Game` keeps the time, refire and count of your last predicted shot per weapon (set where the recoil kick is, in the tick loop), so a part moves on the click like the muzzle flash, never a round trip later. Purely cosmetic and client-side. Drawn on top of the weapon's frame (`weaponFrame`), so the HUD placement, size, tilt and GUN RECOIL KICK apply to the parts as to the body. Spectating someone's first-person gun, and other players' held weapons (third person), draw the parts still at their pivots (we don't see their shots' exact timing there, and the motion is small at that distance).
- **Box models too** (owner, 2026-11-05, picking the outlined look: "just outlined but nicer models"): a box model's box may carry `"part"` (a part name) and `"pivot"`, so outlined box weapons animate exactly like anim_ nodes (`ModelPart::wpart` / `pivot`, read and written by `boxesFrom` / `boxModelJson`; an unknown part or a pivot without a part fails the checks, tested). The first-person box path draws a moving box at frame x scale x pivot x motion x (centre - pivot) through `viewBoxXf` (the outline measured after the scale, as before); a box model with no `part` takes the old path (one scan of its boxes). The shot clock / spin / spring code moved into `Game::viewAnimState`, shared by both paths.
- **Setting:** VIDEO > EFFECTS: WEAPON ANIMATIONS (`weapon_anims`, default on), beside GUN RECOIL KICK (now half width). Off: parts still at their pivots.
- **Cost:** a model without anim nodes takes the same path as before (one `parts.empty()` check). With parts, the first-person weapon draws the body plus one `viewMeshXf` a part (a few 3x4 matrix products: `mulAffine`); the vertices stream as before, so the total vertex count is the same as the whole model. No allocation a frame (parts are built once at load; the draw lists already grow geometrically).

## Player mesh overrides walk (anim_<bone> parts)
- A player mesh override (glTF) whose moving parts are nodes `anim_pelvis` / `anim_torso` / `anim_head` / `anim_thigh_l|r` / `anim_shin_l|r` / `anim_foot_l|r` now walks like the box models (docs/ASSETS.md "Player mesh parts"). - Cost: `Renderer::meshPosed` puts the body and every part into the same dynamic vertex stream as any mesh, so no extra draw call; one frustum test and one shadow caster a player (the whole model's sphere, widened 25% for a stride) instead of one a part; one 3x4 matrix product a part (at most 12) on the stack; no allocation a frame. The vertices transformed are the same count as the model drawn still.

Why box files and not the inverted-hull shells of the first STANDARD sample: players should match the weapons, which the game outlines itself. Shipped: see "Player models: the OUTLINED set is the default" below.
- Each is a few big blocks on a 5 mm grid, no chamfers: 11 to 40 boxes, 132 to 480 triangles. Palette (the outlined weapons' rules): dark = metal slot x 0.55, mid = metal, light = base, recess = metal x 0.25, big team panels (chest, back, shins), one glow piece or two (visors, eyes), plus a few fixed colours a character needs (skin, the zombie's green, the wizard's robe, leather, gold). - The script fails a model with a box point outside the shared hitbox, off the grid, over 1000 triangles or 256 boxes (`kMaxOverrideBoxes`), without a thigh / shin / foot on each side, or under the luminance floor; it warns on two same-facing faces sharing a plane (flicker, doubled outlines). Previews: the lineup in orange and blue (front / back 3/4) and walking poses, drawn with the game's box face factors and 1 px box outlines.
  - The strip lights the tab a screen was opened from (DEMOS from CREATE, CUSTOM MODE from PLAY), and stays off on SETTINGS > HUD, whose HUD preview shows the HUD where it really sits on screen (review round 2 found it drawn under the strip).

## Player models: easier to see (renderer only)
- **Why:** q, 2026-11-10: "the player models by default are hard to see". Measured with an 11-model enemy lineup on sewer, mine, datacenter (dark), war, spire, greenhouse, qk3 (bright / foggy) at 7 to 40 m, 1920x1080: the models' pixels were on average L* 35 with chroma C* 12 (nearly grey), colour difference to what was behind them ΔE 29 (dark maps 24), and on dark maps 37% of a model's pixels were near black (L* < 25; 71% on datacenter at 40 m).
- **Causes found:**
  - The team colour went into the model's slot as it is, but the settings' colours are sRGB and the slots linear: the enemy red (1, 0.3, 0.25) drew as sRGB (255, 148, 136), a pale salmon, and the friendly cyan near white. Now `pow(colour, 2.2)` for players' bodies (game.cpp), so a model shows exactly the HUD's / colour picker's colour.
  - Players took 30% own light (PLAYER BRIGHTNESS 0.30); the previews the outlined set was approved from used 60%. In a dark map a player was lit at about 0.45 of its colour. The default is now 0.60 (`player_emit`), and a config still on the old default 0.30 moves to 0.60 once (`player_emit_v2`); another value is kept.
  - The box outline is a fixed 1 px (+1 px ramp) inside every face's edges. Far away a limb's face is 2-4 px across, so it was all outline: the model turned into a black stick figure. For players the outline now fades with the face's size on screen: full at 12 px and up (so near players look exactly as before), none under 3 px.
  - Fog greyed players like the walls (greenhouse at 20 m: chroma 6). Players take half the fog density now.
- **How:** a player flag in the dynamic vertex's spare normal.w (negative = a player's; it already carried PLAYER SATURATION), set by `Renderer::modelPlayer` while the game draws players (body, gun arm, held weapon, bomb pack). PLAYER BRIGHTNESS (`uEmit`) now applies only to flagged vertices (it brightened every dynamic box: props, the cart, your view model; those keep the old 0.3). No new pass, attribute, texture or uniform per draw: a few ALU ops and one fwidth per player fragment in the existing dynamic pass. Mesh players (no box outline) get the colour, light and fog changes.
- **Result (same spots, same camera):** colour difference model vs background ΔE 29 → 39 (dark maps 24 → 36, 20 m and farther 28 → 38), model chroma C* 12 → 23, pixels clearly distinct (ΔE > 25) 52% → 64%, near-black model pixels on dark maps 37% → 22%.
- **Tools:** `--lineupdist M` (the `--models` lineup M metres ahead, centred, spread wider when far, on the floor; turns you toward the longest open line, or uses `--cam X Y Z YAW 0`, and hides the HUD), `--lineupempty` (the same shot without the lineup, to diff a mask).

## Network: protocol 71, the model index in a whole byte
- EV_PLAYERINFO carried `model | soundPack << 4` in its b byte: 4 bits, 16 models at most. 64 models need 6 bits, the 5 sound packs 3: 9 bits.
- **Chosen:** no new byte. b is now the model alone (8 bits: up to 256), and the sound pack moves into a's top 3 bits over the player id (5 bits: kMaxPlayers is 32; static_asserts guard both). `setPlayerInfoLooks` / `playerInfoId` / `playerInfoModel` / `playerInfoPack` (protocol.h) are the only readers and writers (server, tutorial server, the client, the demo recorder's re-emit). The event layout is unchanged, so every other event and the serializer are untouched; the alternative, an extra u8 for this one event type, would have made the generic event layout type-dependent for one byte per name / model change.
- **Protocol 71** (70 was the latest on testing and every open branch): a 70 reader would split a 71 model byte as model | pack and read a's pack bits as the id.
- **Demos:** the converter rewrites each EV_PLAYERINFO of a protocol <= 70 demo into 71's layout (`upgradePlayerInfo`: same size, written over the event part in place; snapshots without one are copied as before). Test: a protocol 70 record (player 4, NINJA, pack 3) converts and reads back as such, plus a model past 15 and an out-of-range model / pack.

## Maps: the build runs the map editor's fixers on every generated map
- **Why:** q, 2026-11-10: "apply the fixes, zfighting fix, etc to all of [the maps]": the Quarkology map editor's tidy buttons (Fix z-fighting, Fix map, Fix gaps, Fix spawns). Generated maps are build output (`.qmap` files are never edited by hand), so the fixers now run inside the build: `tools/maps/tidy.py`, called by `M.save` (`tools/maps/prefabs.py`) on each generated map's own pieces before it is written. Hand-made maps (accelerator, amphitheatre, museum, reactors, riverside, surfy, cobblestone) and the owner's overrides ship byte for byte as before; the legacy maps (`maps/legacy/`) are left as they were (`prefabs.TIDY` off for them); a map can opt out with `no_tidy = True` on its builder.
- **What it does,** in order, again until nothing changes (a port of the editor's `fixers.js` / `fixmap.js` / `csg.js`, same tolerances):
  1. **Z-fighting:** two pieces that overlap and share a face plane facing the same way flicker there. As the editor does, the smaller piece's box is carved out of the bigger one (exact, up to 6 slabs) and an exact duplicate goes. New here: the other way round (the smaller one loses what is inside the bigger one: "trimmed") where carving would change what a shot goes through (map.h `wallbangBrush`: a piece 1/8 m thin or less is shot through, so a thin trim strip run through a thick pillar would leave a slot only the strip fills, or a slab of the wall could come out 1/8 m thin), and where trimming makes fewer pieces and keeps some of the smaller piece (a rung across a post, a gravel patch run into a rock wall: carving would cut the post or the wall up at every one: carving only, as the editor does, made Mine 2,036 pieces, Castle 2,257, Datacenter 1,558 and Dojo 638, against 1,386, 1,693, 1,191 and 522 now). Trimming never removes a piece that is wholly inside the other (an inlaid decal is carved in, as the editor does).
  2. **Merge** (Fix map): plain boxes of one material that make exactly one box become one.
  3. **Hidden** (Fix map): a box whose every face is covered by solid opaque boxes goes, but only when those boxes also fill it (the editor's test alone would leave a sealed void in 4 maps: 10 boxes kept).
  4. **Gaps** (Fix gaps, 1/8 m grid): faces under 1/8 m apart are closed by moving the one off the grid out, plain boxes only (as the game's own FIX GAPS: stretching a slope would tilt it), and only when the face that moves lies within the face it meets, so it just fills the slot between them, and nothing else, a staircase or a ladder is in the way. The editor moves the whole face: on Convoy that raised the top of a stair's last step from 4.41 to 4.5 m where it was walked on, and `quark_tests maps`' stairs check failed (a floor's top must never rise where it's stood on, a wall never step out into the open). 590 gaps closed.
  5. **Spawns** (Fix spawns): a spawn whose hull is in something solid would go up 1/8 m at a time (none is today; one still stuck after 64 m stops the build).
- **Gameplay is unchanged** by steps 1-3: at every point the solid space, what stops a shot (not wallbang) and every material that does something are the same as before. So pairs are left as they are (counted in the report) where either piece is a material that acts: water, lava, the liquids, acid, ice, rubber, asphalt, mud, nograpple, wallbang, clip, the ball wall, glass, breakables, the see-through looks, the feature blocks, teleporters (their links and order), checkpoint gates (their count). Liquids, lava and acid may merge (each box is only asked whether you touch it); a breakable, a crumble platform, a light block, a teleporter, a gate or a gravity flip never merges. Slopes and cut boxes are never changed. The gaps step is the one that changes the solid space, by design: slots under 1/8 m between two pieces, that no player fits in and shots could slip through; liquids, lava and acid never grow into one, and no piece grows where it would be stood on or walked into. Checked by sampling 40 points in every changed piece of every map before and after (zero differences for steps 1-3) and against the editor's own fixers run on the new maps (below).
- **Pieces:** 50,405 -> 48,765 over the 35 generated maps (-3.3%). Biggest drops: Bridge 1,340 -> 903 (432 hidden boxes), Datacenter 1,478 -> 1,191, Railyard 1,575 -> 1,273, QK2 375 -> 301, QK6 490 -> 398, War 2,062 -> 1,929, Mine 1,484 -> 1,386. Biggest rises: QK3 150 -> 164 (+9%), Orbit 204 -> 219, Dojo 490 -> 522 (+7%), Dam 869 -> 897, Express 912 -> 934. No map grew more than 9%.
- **What the editor still finds on the new maps** (its dry runs, default options): no hidden boxes, merges or stuck spawns but where listed here; the z-fighting pairs and gaps it would still fix are all ones this pass leaves on purpose: a gameplay material (War 105 pairs, Cruiseship 78, Solar 56, Fairground 50, Orbit 24, Datacenter 23, Sewer and Stadium 17-18, Bridge 17, Mine 11, a few elsewhere), a cut that can't keep what is shot through either way (Fairground 35, Bridge 8, Dam 4, a few single ones), a slope's face (the editor's gap fix stretches slopes: Fairground, Greenhouse, Lighthouse, Terraces, Sewer, Railyard), a gap the editor closes by moving a face bigger than the one it meets (Cruiseship 357, Rooftops 331, Datacenter 100, Fairground 54, Mine 40, Convoy 30, a few to 24 elsewhere), or that would grow a gameplay piece (Mine 28, Castle 14, Sewer and Dam 8) or make a thin wallbang piece thick (Stadium 4). ## Renderer: z-fighting of see-through and shaped pieces (q, 2026-11-10: "fix z-fighting on every map")
- **Why:** after the map fixer pass and the world mesh's own lift (step 1c: the thinner of two flush faces of different looks lifted 4 mm), two renderer gaps still flickered on every map. (1) See-through things (glass and windows, zone boxes and the like in the flat pass; the see-through looks such as chain-link, fields and shield walls in their own pass) draw blended with no depth write and `GL_LEQUAL`. They were never moved off a coplanar opaque face, so a pane flush with or overlapping its frame, a sill or a wall showed a speckled tint: Museum's skylight, Reactors' glass room, Greenhouse's partition panes on their sills, every window set flush in a wall. (2) Faces of shaped pieces (wedges, corners, tetras, cut boxes) were skipped by step 1c, so a slope face coplanar with another piece flickered.
- **(1), `src/client/renderer.cpp`:** both see-through passes draw with `glPolygonOffset(1, 2)`.
  - **What it does:** pushes them a hair AWAY from the camera, in depth-buffer steps, so the bias grows with distance exactly as the depth buffer's own precision does. Where they lie on an opaque face, the opaque face wins every pixel.
  - **Away, not toward:** a pane overlapping its frame is inside the frame there, so the frame shows untinted. Biasing toward the camera would tint the frame and could show glass through a wall in front of it at distance.
  - **Cost:** 4 GL state calls a frame. No extra pass, draw or vertex.
  - **Trade-off:** a see-through face closer than about 2 depth steps in front of an opaque face (2.4 cm at 100 m, 0.2 m at 300 m) hides behind it. Such faces were z-fighting before anyway.
  - **Checked:** Cruiseship's windows 60 m away still draw and tint.
- **(2), `src/client/lightmap.cpp` step 1c:** slope polygons take part in the lift.
  - **Depth:** each gets the depth the "thinner piece goes on top" rule needs: how far its brush's box reaches behind its plane.
  - **Axis planes:** a slope polygon on an axis plane (a wedge's side) is found in the bucket grid like a box face. A pair overlaps only where a 4x4 grid over their rectangles' intersection lies inside both, never because their bounding rectangles do, so no lift appears where the faces only touch along an edge (the 2026-10-31 rule against lifting faces that merely touch).
  - **Sloped planes:** they are matched by plane: the normal to 1/100 and the distance to 1/2 m, with the neighbouring distance buckets. All against all took 8.6 s per load on Solar (39,000 sloped polygons). Accelerator gets the same 40 lifts either way.
  - **The lift:** the same 4 mm a level, now also applied to a slope polygon's corners.
- **Load-time cost (step 1c runs on every load, cached light or not):**
  - Later passes skip the faces that overlapped nothing in the first pass.
  - A huge face (over 32x32 cells of 8 m) checks only the faces on its own plane, not every face of the map.
  - Solar: step 1c took 230 ms before and about 145 ms now with the shaped pieces included (67 ms for the old work alone). Other maps: 1-10 ms.
  - The light cache holds only texels and is unchanged, so no bake version bump is needed.
- **Numbers:** lifted shaped faces per map, e.g. Accelerator 333 (40 on sloped planes), Terraces 259, Sewer 101, Monorail 50, Greenhouse 37, Solar 104.
- **Test:** `quark_tests groupslopes` checks a wedge flush with a box of another look. With the box deeper than the wedge, the wedge's side is lifted 4 mm; with the box thinner, the box's face is.

## Ball: step height
- Owner, 2026-11-10: "ball should have a step height". `ballMove` (pmove.cpp) bounced the ball off every non-slope face it ran into on x or z, so a 0.2 m curb or a stair's riser sent it back at half speed like a wall.
- Now, before the rubber bounce, it tries a player's step: up by up to `kStepHeight` (0.55 m, the player's base step), the rest of that axis's move, then back down by what it rose. It takes the step only when that gets it further along and leaves it higher (a riser, not a flat wall). Its velocity is kept: it goes over the lip at the speed it hit with, as a player does. A face taller than 0.55 m still bounces it.
- Applies on the floor and in the air (a ball clipping a ledge's lip in flight goes over it), the same as a player's air step. Slopes are unchanged (already turned along the face). Ball walls are tall, so the ball never steps over one.
- Server only, like the rest of the ball's physics (the ball isn't predicted): no protocol change. Cost: three extra axis sweeps only on a 1 ms step where the ball hits a face.
- Test: `quark_tests ball` rolls it at 6 m/s into a 0.5 m step (it ends on top, still moving forward) and a 0.7 m ledge (it bounces off).

## Sounds: the sound audit's cues (owner, 2026-10-30: "All of them")
- **Why:** the sound audit's P1 list: moments that had no sound of their own, or reused one that meant something else (the planted bomb beeped with the sticky's attach sound pitched up; a checkpoint that doesn't count dinged like one that does; a headshot was the light hitsound pitched to 1.7). q approved all nine.
- **Nine new sounds,** synthesized in `audio.cpp` like every DEFAULT sound, added after SIZZLE (before the announcer's lines) so the recorded kits' ids (sfx_recorded.h) and everyone's overrides keep their meaning; the other kits play DEFAULT's (RETRO chiptuned), and each is overridable as `sounds/<kit>/<NAME>.wav`. 43 sounds a kit, 215 in all (was 34 / 170). Provenance, the custom-sounds README, ASSETS.md and HANDOVER updated.
  - `HEADSHOT_TINK` (0.2 s): inharmonic partials at 3.87, 5.42 and 8.13 kHz (a small bell) over a 2 ms noise click.
  - `HOOKED` (0.45 s): a 230 -> 90 Hz thwack with a noise snap, then a whine rising 380 -> 950 Hz.
  - `BOMB_TICK` (0.09 s): a 2.09 kHz beep with its octave and a click.
  - `BOMB_PLANTING` (0.1 s): a 1.56 kHz keypad beep and click; `BOMB_DEFUSING` (0.12 s): a lower 900 -> 560 Hz tool click with a scrape of high noise.
  - `HILL_CONTESTED` (0.5 s): 880 / 660 / 880 Hz, 0.12 s apart, each with a third harmonic.
  - `CAPTURE_ALARM` (0.75 s): two siren whoops rising 520 -> 1150 Hz, 0.3 s apart.
  - `HOLDFIRE_TICK` (0.1 s): a wooden clock tick (1.8 -> 1.15 kHz knock and noise).
  - `CHECKPOINT_WRONG` (0.38 s): a square-ish 175 Hz buzz (odd harmonics to the 7th), twice.
- **All client-side, no protocol change** (the 1.18 sound fixes' rule: from the snapshots and the events already sent). The pure rules are in `modecalls.h` (`bombTickInterval` / `bombTickPitch`, `bombActionOf`, `hillContested`, `pointUnderCapture`, `CueGate`, `holdFireCue`, `WrongCheckpoint`); the plays in `Game::updateAnnouncer` (bomb, hill, point, holdfire), `Game::updateSounds` (hooked, wrong checkpoint), `Game::hitFeedback` (headshot) and `Game::onRaceSplit` (a run that doesn't count).
  - **Headshot:** EV_HIT already says a hit was a headshot (text "h") and the game's own hit reports know it, so `hitFeedback` plays the tink in place of the pitched-up light hitsound, still under the HEADSHOT setting (HITSOUNDS) and the hitsound's pack and volume. Hits landing within the hitsound gate's 40 ms make one tink.
  - **Hooked:** your movement state's `MF_PULLED` (protocol 38) going on, once; not again within 0.5 s (a break free the server hasn't confirmed can flicker it).
  - **Bomb tick:** the same rate as the bomb model's blink (fuse left / 20 s, at least 0.15 s: 71 ticks over a 40 s fuse), its pitch rising 1.0 -> 1.3 over the last 5 s, 3D at the bomb, under BOMB BEEP volume.
  - **Plant / defuse:** a click every 0.3 s while `ModeState::actionPlayer` is set: planting 3D at the planter, defusing at the bomb. The server already clears `actionPlayer` for a client that can't see the one acting, so nothing is heard through walls that the client isn't sent.
  - **Hill contested:** `MS_CONTESTED` in king of the hill turning on, at most every 4 s (it flickers as players step on and off). **Capture alarm:** multi point, your team defending, the attackers alone on the point (`hillOwner` = attackers while they fill it, not contested): at once, then every 3 s while it lasts. Both 2D, for every player on a team.
  - **Holdfire:** from the client's own `holdFireLeft()`: a tick as each second begins (5, 4, 3, 2, 1 on Dojo's 5 s) and the release as it reaches 0 (the tick at 1.5x pitch, louder); none if the round ends inside it.
  - **Wrong checkpoint:** race and its practice, your predicted box entering a checkpoint past the next one the server will count (`PlayerSnap::checkpoint`): one buzz as you enter it. The next one just entered counts as taken until the server's count catches up, so the gate after it never buzzes for lag. A run joined midway (`onRaceSplit`'s "this run doesn't count") buzzes instead of dinging. Cost: an AABB test per checkpoint per frame, only in race modes.
- **The bomb's flash** (it had only the boom): `Game::bombBlast` adds two of the existing explosion boxes at the bomb, a fireball growing to the kill radius (18 m) over 1 s and a 0.5 s core, and a white screen flash up to 0.7 opacity fading over 0.6 s, scaled by distance (none past 2.5 x the radius) and by a third behind a wall (one map ray, once a round). REDUCE FLASHING: no screen flash, and the fireball is softer like every explosion; EFFECTS OPACITY scales both.
- **Performance:** no allocations a frame; each cue is a few compares on state already there. The bomb's flash pushes two entries into the explosions list once a round.
