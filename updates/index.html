1.19.3Latest

Everything from the testing builds since 1.19.1 (1.19.2 was never released on its own, so its changes ship here): new map pieces (wedges, corners, tetras and cut boxes at any angle), 472 map materials (ice, rubber, glass you can shatter, brick walls you can blow open, and 433 new looks: brick, concrete, tile, stone, nature, buildings, industrial, sci-fi and wasteland), plain and breakable versions of brick, cobblestone and glass, a new surf mode with its map Cascade, damage from your own screen (hits count where you saw them), PRIORITIZE GAME on Windows, and the map editor's material picker, FIX MAP and FIX GAPS. The network protocol is now 53 (1.19.1 used 47): 1.19.3 doesn't play with 1.19.1 games or servers, so update both. Dedicated servers update themselves. Maps saved by 1.19.3 are format 10: 1.19.1 can't open them; older maps convert by themselves. Your settings, maps, demos and files are kept. Human-made assets: 16.1%.

Performance

  • New Settings > VIDEO > PRIORITIZE GAME (Windows, on by default): while the game has focus, Windows runs its GPU work before other programs' (a YouTube tab, Discord or other hardware-accelerated apps) and gives it a higher CPU priority. Before, a video playing in the background could take a big share of every frame. Alt-tab and the other programs get their normal share back.
  • Blown-open brick and cobblestone walls no longer cut your FPS: after holes were blown in a wall (or the whole wall fell), every brick and cobblestone pixel on screen kept checking every hole on the map, so a room of brick could drop to a tenth of the frame rate. Each pixel now checks only the holes near it.

Maps

  • Wedges and corners: two new map pieces besides boxes. A wedge is a ramp (one top edge kept), a corner a pyramid with one top corner kept; the slope is whatever the box gives, so any angle. They can stand on the floor, hang upside down from a ceiling or lie on their side (an angled wall). Slopes up to 45 degrees are walkable (no hopping), steeper ones are walls you slide off or along; a dash into one rides up it (or down it). Shots, the grapple and light all follow the slope, and bots walk up gentle ones. In the map editor, N cycles a box through them and rotate / mirror turn them any way; Quarkology's map editor has Wedge and Corner buttons. Wedges and corners can be grouped with boxes, and the seams between pieces of one group go away, slopes included. Maps with them are format 5 (older maps load unchanged); older game versions can't join servers running this one (protocol 48).
  • Fewer stray outlines: where a box's edge was only partly against a flush neighbour (a taller step next to a shorter one, a wall meeting a longer wall), the whole edge kept its line; now only the uncovered part does. In hand-made maps, outlines now follow groups: parts of the same group join with no line, even across materials, and separate groups or ungrouped boxes keep theirs. The official maps keep joining same-material boxes.
  • FIX MAP and FIX GAPS in the map editor: FIX MAP joins same-material boxes that together make one box and removes boxes you can never see (wholly inside solid walls; anything behind glass, water or breakable brick stays). FIX GAPS closes gaps under 1/8 m by stretching the box that's off the grid to the one on it. Each is one undo step.
  • No more holes into the sky behind blown-open brick: a wall face covered by a thin brick or cobblestone layer was left out of the world, so blowing the brick open showed through to nothing. Those faces are kept now.
  • Cobblestone looks solid: dark streaks ran out of every corner where three stones met, which looked like gaps in the wall, and each stone was shaded in squares. The joints are now an even width and each stone has one shade.
  • Material picker in the map editor: clicking a material (for new boxes, or for what's selected) opens a grid of swatches, each drawn in the game's own look and the map's colours. It has three lists: the materials this map already uses, the last 10 you picked (kept between sessions) and all of them. M still cycles. FIX MAP / FIX GAPS now show only when nothing is selected, so the selection's buttons fit on screen.
  • 100 new brick, concrete, tile and stone materials (all of the picks): 30 bricks (colours plus Flemish, English, stack, soldier, herringbone and basket bonds), 20 concretes, 25 tiles and 25 stone walls, each drawn by the game with no textures. The map editor's material picker now fits all of them in one grid, with the name of the one under the mouse shown by CLOSE.
  • 333 more materials: your picks from the four earlier rounds of surface looks: 77 general ones (dirt, gravel, slate, basalt, hedge, coral, sci-fi panels and more), 100 nature, 86 industrial and 70 wasteland. All are solid and plain, each drawn by the game with no textures. A map's shader only carries the looks that map uses, so having 472 materials costs nothing in a match.
  • Bigger material picker: each swatch is now 100 × 100 with its name under it. Tabs split the materials by kind (IN THIS MAP, RECENT, BASIC, BRICK, CONCRETE, TILE, STONE, BUILDINGS, NATURE, INDUSTRIAL, SCI-FI / DECOR, WASTELAND, ALL), SEARCH finds them by name, and the mouse wheel scrolls.
  • Breakable and plain brick, cobblestone and glass are now separate materials: plain brick, cobblestone and glass never break, so a plain wall costs nothing extra (no hole checks, nothing kept behind it, and FIX MAP can merge it and hide what's behind it). BREAKABLE BRICK, BREAKABLE COBBLESTONE (thin ones blow open) and BREAKABLE GLASS work as before. Maps made before this convert by themselves: their glass and their thin brick and cobblestone become the breakable kind, so they play the same. Older game versions can't join servers running this one (protocol 49).

Weapons

  • Rifle: +1 damage a shot: 6 body (was 5), 8.5 head (was 7.5). A kill takes 17 body shots in 2.4 s (was 20 in 2.9 s), or 12 headshots in 1.7 s.
  • Bubble gun fires 20% faster: 6 bubbles a second (was 5), so 10 pops kill in 1.5 s (was 1.8 s).
  • Bubble gun alt-fire: up to 25 bubbles a stream from 30 m away (was 15). Still 5 when the nearest enemy is within 5 m, and the same 1.8 s reload.
  • 33% more knockback from the shotgun (a full blast shoves 8 m/s, was 6) and the crossbow (21.3 m/s a bolt, was 16).
  • Sniper: 33% more knockback (1.33 m/s a hit, was 1).

Movement

  • Dashing up goes higher: looking straight up, 50% of a dash goes upward (was 40%).
  • Air dashes add more speed the slower you are: a dash in the air now adds 35% of the gap between your speed and the 32 m/s cap. From 10 m/s you leave at 17.7 (was 11), from 25 at 27.5 (was 26), from a standstill at 11.2. Dashes on the floor and super dashes (hypers) are unchanged.

Downloads

  • Smaller game downloads in the launcher (coming): every release now also publishes a compressed copy of the game package, about half the size (Windows: about 40 MB instead of 72). The launcher starts using it once its own update ships; until then nothing changes, and the old package stays for older launchers.
  • Releases no longer wait for a Mac: GitHub often has no Mac free for hours, which held releases back (1.19.1 waited for one). Windows and Linux now go out as soon as they're built, and the macOS files follow when a Mac is free.
  • Surf: a new mode. Race rules (checkpoints in order, the first to the finish wins the round) on a course of steep ramps, with no dash and no grapple: you ride a ramp's face by strafing into it. Its map is Cascade, four stages of clean 52 and 60 degree ridges down a frozen gorge: gaps to fly across, a turn, zig-zags and two kickers that throw you up onto a ledge if you're fast enough. A dash into any slope now keeps its whole speed and turns along it. In surf you only hold the knife and nobody can hurt anybody (warmup too). Ramps are half as slippery: on a ramp's face you fall (and slide down and off it) at half speed. Strafing into a ramp you touch grips 2x harder (the air speed cap is unchanged).
  • New materials: ice (slippery underfoot), rubber (you bounce off it, floors, walls and ceilings, and it grips: faster to speed up and to stop), and sand, wood, plastic, grass, steel and diamond plate (they play like base, each with its own look). Metal now looks like brushed metal with a sheen, and water, lava and acid have moving noise across them, so lava reads as lava. Every look is made by the shader: no texture files, no extra memory.
  • Rockets and stickies bounce off rubber; stickies and a dropped bomb slide on ice, and a dropped bomb bounces on rubber. The grapple holds on ice as before.
  • Map editor: a new box is one grid cell (grid 0.25 m: a 0.25 m cube).
  • More materials: brick (classic red), glass, asphalt, mud, cobblestone, concrete, tile, marble, corrugated metal, hazard stripes, snow, stone blocks, crystal (glowing edges), circuit (pulsing traces), rust and terracotta. Grass is now green on top and down the top 1/4 m of its sides wherever nothing sits on it. Asphalt is fast and grippy, mud is sticky (slower, stops you hard, weaker jumps), and a dash started on ice is only 25% as strong.
  • Glass breaks: it's see-through and solid (you can stand on it and stickies sit on it) until it breaks. A shot or the knife leaves a crater and breaks the pane into 10-20 big shards, the ones near the crater flying on through it; a bolt or a blast (not the bubble) shatters the whole pane into 12-40 big pieces flying away from the blast. Dashing through shatters it along the dash, and grappling to it shatters it as you arrive; both cost you a flat 10 and its shards spare you. Grapple a pane while standing or moving away and the shards come back at you. Every flying shard or piece does 10 damage. Blast shards fly 3x as far, dash shards 2x, and the rest 1.5x; once they land they're harmless, and they vanish once they're out of view. Your own breaks show at once.
  • Blow holes in walls: brick or cobblestone no thicker than 1/8 m blows open from a rocket or sticky, a 2.5 m hole of broken bricks from a direct hit, smaller the further away the blast was (1 m away: 1.5 m). Nothing else breaks it.
  • Broken glass and holes come back every round, at a new match and on a new map (FFA keeps them until then).
  • Lava, water, acid and the patterned surfaces no longer shimmer seen from a low angle or far off: detail finer than about 2 pixels fades into its average.
  • Your hits count as you saw them: hitscan shots, rockets and crossbow bolts now do damage where they hit on your screen. Before, the server checked them again, so at high ping a rocket or bolt that clearly hit could do nothing. The only exception is when you die on the server before your hit reaches it. Your own rocket on yourself (rocket jumps) and the shotgun's ricochet are still the server's, and so are stickies, bubbles, bots and the grapple.
  • Tetra: a new map piece: a three-sided pyramid, with three base corners and its peak over one of them (a wedge corner cut in half along its diagonal). It walks, collides, lights and renders like the other slopes, and bots walk on gentle ones. In the map editor, N cycles through it after the corners, and rotate / mirror turn it any way.
  • Cut box: a new map piece: a box cut by up to six flat planes at any angle. Wedges, corners and tetras are simple cases of it. A round dome is now exact: each face is one cut box, with no gaps and nothing sticking out. It walks, collides, lights and renders like the other slopes, and the map editor's turn / mirror keys turn it too.
  • Coat in the map editor: select a box, look at one of its sides and press K to lay a 1/8 m layer of the current material on it (with several boxes selected, on that side of each). Good for a thin brick or glass skin on a wall.
  • Fixed: your rocket bouncing off rubber went invisible on your screen until the server's next update; it's now drawn along its bounce. Shooting a pane now brings the whole pane down in visible pieces (only the shards blown out of the bullet hole hurt), instead of a few tiny shards flying away from you.
  • Walls come down: once 80% of a thin brick or cobblestone wall has been blown away, the rest of it falls in a burst of chunks. It's back next round, like the holes.
  • Brighter glow: glow trim, crystal edges and circuit traces are brighter.
  • 25 liquids (your picks): lagoon, swamp, oil slick, tar, honey, mercury, molten gold, magma, toxic sludge, blood pool, hot spring, quicksand, ice slush, plasma pool, ink, glowing tide, sewage, wet cement, pink slime, coolant, white rapids, deep ocean, soda, void and mud bath. You wade into all of them, and each plays its own way: honey, tar and quicksand slow you and hold you back, oil is slippery, coolant speeds you up, the rapids carry you along, pink slime throws a fall back up, soda gives big jumps, deep ocean and quicksand change your fall, a hot spring heals you, toxic sludge and plasma hurt, and molten gold, magma and void kill like lava. They're under the new LIQUIDS tab in the map editor.
  • Map editor: the material picker fills the whole window, with previews twice as big (and sharp). New SPLIT, COMBINE and SUBTRACT buttons for a selected box: SPLIT cuts the selection in two along its longest side, COMBINE joins boxes of one material that make one clean box (anything else becomes a group), and SUBTRACT carves the first box you selected out of the others (shift-click them), so a doorway leaves the wall's pieces round the hole.
  • 40 large patterns (new materials, LARGE tab): the same looks with pieces 2 to 4 times as big: wide and huge planks, large and huge brick, cobbles, tile and stone, and large versions of 30 more brick, tile, stone, sett, flagstone and wood looks. They play like the normal ones.
  • Glass breaks two ways: a shot (or a grapple pulled back) SHARDS it: 2-5 big shards a square metre, 10 damage each, with a spray of glitter where it was hit. A blast, a dash or being pulled through SHATTERS it: 25-100 small pieces a square metre, 1 damage each, in a cloud of 250-1000 glitter bits. Glitter never hurts.
  • 25 see-through blocks (SEE-THROUGH tab): tinted, smoked, amber, frosted, reeded, stained and rain-streaked glass, glass block, plexiglass, ice, quartz, green gel, a hex force field, a red laser barrier, a hologram, chain-link mesh, cargo net, soap film, a water curtain, a smoke wall, prism glass, a jelly cube, ghost glass, a scanner beam and bottle glass. Each is drawn with its own pattern and you see through it. The glass ones stop you and your shots like a window. The force field stops players but lets shots through, the laser barrier is the other way round, a mesh or net lets shots through its holes, smoke blocks only sight, and you walk straight through the water curtain, hologram, soap film and scanner beam. Frosted glass hides you from bots.
  • 24 breakable blocks (BREAKABLE tab): wooden crate, plywood board-up, drywall, cinder block, paper screen, bamboo and picket fences, cardboard boxes, chipboard, rusted sheet, thin concrete, crystal, sandstone, sugar glass, hay bales, rotten planks, ceramic tiles, mirror, barricade, frozen waterfall, stained glass window, tyre stack, lath and plaster, and glass bottles. Each takes a set amount of damage before it breaks into chunks. A crate goes after one rocket, concrete panels and hay shrug off bullets, cinder blocks and tyres take a lot, and a mirror breaks after a couple of shots. Sugar glass, paper and cardboard break the moment you touch them, plywood, drywall and picket fences when you dash into them, and rotten planks give way a moment after you stand on them. They're all back next round.
  • 10 feature blocks (FEATURES tab):
    • Healing tile heals 10% of your health a second while you stand on it. Spike floor takes 20% a second.
    • Low gravity field halves gravity inside it.
    • Slow-mo field makes rockets, stickies, bolts and other projectiles fly at half speed inside it.
    • Gravity flip: above it, gravity points up. You fall to the ceiling and can walk and jump on it.
    • Checkpoint gate is a race checkpoint you run through. It comes after the map's own checkpoints, and the last one is the finish. It flashes green when you take it.
    • Light block glows in cycling colours and lights the room around it.
    • Shield wall stops bullets, rockets and grapples, but you can walk and see through it.
    • Crumble platform shakes when you step on it, falls a second later, and comes back 5 seconds after that.
    • Teleporter has a LINK number and an EXIT FACE, both set in the editor. Touch one and you come out of the next teleporter with the same LINK, on its exit face, at the same speed. Rockets and other projectiles go through too.
  • Strafing at speed: A/D strafing loses less of its push as you go faster. The window of angles that gain is the same, but speed keeps climbing for longer. In surf it falls off much less, so you can go very fast. A/D strafing also turns you about 50% harder without adding speed. A perfect strafer from 15 m/s reaches 47 m/s after 5 s (was 41) and 88 after 15 s (was 71); in surf, 56 and 123.
  • Glass shards from one broken pane hurt you 50 at most, however many hit you.
  • Free for all rulesets: free for all can be played with any of the 1v1 rulesets (vanilla, laser vampire, classic, quirky, hitscan, instagib, boomers, blades, moon shotgun). Pick one after choosing FREE FOR ALL when hosting, against bots, or in a map vote or change in game. Vanilla plays as free for all always has.
  • Gravity is heavier (24, was 22). Jump speed went up with it, so a standing jump is still 1.22 m high, but you spend about 4% less time in the air (a running jump reaches 6.0 m, was 6.3).
  • F3 cycles: press it once for the FPS line alone, twice for FPS plus worst frame, ping, 3D size and server rate, three times for everything with the latency graph, and a fourth time to turn it off.
  • Chat in settings: the chat stays on screen while the settings menu is open in a game, and Enter opens it there so you can type.

Everything that changed in 1.19.3, and why →

1.19.1

Everything from the testing builds since 1.19.0: a grapple launcher on everyone's arm and rocket riding, impact sparks and muzzle flashes, explosions that show at once, a tighter head hitbox, two new map materials (NOGRAPPLE and ACID), new and more detailed player and weapon models, an on-screen HUD editor with HUD styles, RENDER SCALE, a built-in BENCHMARK, and more optimized builds of the game with an AVX2 version picked by itself. The network protocol is now 47 (1.19.0 used 44): 1.19.1 doesn't play with 1.19.0 games or servers, so update both. Dedicated servers update themselves. Maps saved by 1.19.1 are format 4: 1.19.0 can't open them. Your settings, maps, demos and files are kept.

Testing builds

  • Testing builds are now numbered 1.19.1.1, 1.19.1.2 and so on (tag test-1.19.1.<N>): the production version they follow plus a count that starts at 1. The launcher's TESTING channel and the testing servers see them as newer than 1.19.1 and older than the next release, so there's nothing to do as a player. (Between 1.19.0 and 1.19.1 they were 1.19.0.testing.<run>.)
  • Server owners, once: a testing server's updater from before this change doesn't know the new numbers and stops the testing server once 1.19.1 is out. Run sudo sh tools/server/install.sh once from a checkout of v1.19.1 (or the testing branch), as for 1.19.0 (docs/OPERATIONS.md). It's the last time: from now on servers keep up with every update by themselves.

Grapple

  • Everyone now wears a grapple launcher on their left arm, in first person (low on the left, like the gun on the right) and on other players. The rope comes out of its barrel, and the hook's claw flies out to where it latched; while you're hooked your arm lifts toward it. It has its own HUD > GRAPPLE MODEL setting and isn't shown in modes without the grapple.
  • The gun and the grapple launcher now sit further back in the bottom corners by default, covering less of the screen, and in the same place at every aspect ratio (16:9, 4:3, ultrawide) and FOV.
  • New HUD > GRAPPLE MODEL setting: move, size, hide and colour the launcher on its own, separately from the WEAPON MODEL.
  • Tilt for both models: PITCH, YAW and ROLL sliders (-90 to 90 degrees) next to x / y / depth, so each can be placed on all 6 axes. Your shots and the rope leave the moved, tilted barrels.
  • Rocket riding: the grapple now catches rockets in flight, anyone's, your own too: fire a rocket, hook it and it pulls you along behind it. Only rockets (not knives, syringes, stickies or bolts). The hook lets go when the rocket explodes or after the usual 3 s, range and line of sight limits, you keep your speed and the grapple's cooldown starts. Custom modes can turn it off (GENERAL > ABILITIES > ROCKET RIDE); it's on everywhere else. A new network protocol (47): servers and players need this version.

Shots and explosions

  • Impact sparks everywhere: hitscan shots (rifle, sniper, shotgun, laser) now spark where they hit a wall, not only on enemies, and other players' shots spark too. Settings > GAMEPLAY > SHOTS > IMPACT SPARKS turns them off.
  • Muzzle flash on your gun and everyone else's when they fire. Settings > GAMEPLAY > SHOTS > MUZZLE FLASH turns it off.
  • Your explosions show at once: your rockets and bolts blow up on your screen the moment they hit a wall or an enemy, and your stickies the moment you detonate, instead of a round trip later. Damage and deaths still come from the server, so nobody looks dead who isn't.
  • Screenshots no longer overwrite each other: the screenshot key (F12) now saves to a screenshots folder in your game data folder, named by date and time, and the notice says the file name. Before, every launch started again at screenshot_0.png and replaced the last session's.
  • Weapon model opacity: Settings > GAMEPLAY > VIEW > WEAPON MODEL OPACITY makes your first-person gun and grapple launcher see-through (1 = solid, 0 = gone).
  • More detailed weapon models: every first-person weapon except the locator is rebuilt with more parts (sights, rails, grips, glowing details), up to 20 boxes each. Their size and where they sit on screen are unchanged.
  • New player models: WIZARD, ZOMBIE and FIGHTER, plus SIMPLE (just the hitbox: a body box and a head box), which replaces SCOUT (anyone who picked SCOUT now gets SIMPLE). STANDARD, HEAVY, ROBOT and KNIGHT and every projectile (rocket, sticky, bolt, knife, syringe, bubble, orb) are rebuilt with more detail. Every model still has the same hitbox.
  • More player models: PIRATE, NINJA and BANDIT.
  • New map material: NOGRAPPLE (slate blue with diagonal stripes). It is solid like a normal wall for players, shots and sight, but your grapple hits it and lets go instead of latching on. In the editor it comes after CLIP on the M key and the material list. Maps saved by this version are format 3; older games cannot open maps that use it.
  • New map material: ACID (glowing toxic green). You wade into it like lava, but instead of killing you at once it takes 10% of your max health when you touch it, then 10% a second while you stay in, so anyone lasts about 9 seconds whatever the mode's health. Your health drops at once on your screen; the death still comes from the server ("dissolved" in the kill feed), and whoever knocked you in gets the kill however long it takes. Regeneration waits while you're in it, and bots walk round it when they can. In the editor it comes after NOGRAPPLE. Maps saved by this version are format 4; this is a new network protocol (45), so servers and players need this version.
  • The head hitbox is just the head: a player is now hit as two boxes, the body (full width, from the feet up to the bottom of the head) and the head on top. The space beside the head, above the shoulders, is no longer part of the hitbox, so a shot past someone's ear misses instead of counting as a body hit. The same for every weapon, projectile, the knife and the grapple, crouched or any size. Blasts measure to the body or the head too, and bots aim a little lower so they keep hitting the body. This is a new network protocol (46), so servers and players need this version.
  • Fixed missing box faces: some faces of stairs and other stacked boxes were invisible in game. The check that hides faces buried inside other boxes was approximate and could hide a face that was still partly visible; it is now exact, and invisible clip boxes no longer hide the faces behind them.

Sounds

  • The bomb going off now makes a sound: a deep, long boom at the bomb that everyone hears. Before, the round just ended in silence.
  • Tag passes are heard: being tagged plays a stab and a sting, tagging someone plays a heavy hit sound (with HITSOUND on), and everyone else hears the new "it" get hit.
  • Race practice checkpoints ding (CHECKPOINT and FINISH), like in the race itself. Before, practice laps before the race were silent.
  • Demos: the recorded player's own shots are heard again. Before, their gunfire was silent in playback.

HUD

  • Edit the HUD on screen: Settings > HUD > EDIT ON SCREEN shows every HUD element where it goes, with the score box, kill feed and chat too. Click one to select it, drag it to move it (it snaps to the screen's edges and centre and to the other elements, with guide lines; hold Alt to place it freely), and drag a corner, use the wheel or press + / - to size it. Arrow keys nudge it (Shift: 10 at a time), Tab picks the next element, Delete hides or shows it, and Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo. A small panel beside it has the element's settings and its style, plus RESET ELEMENT, RESET ALL and DONE.
  • HUD styles (theme.cfg): give any HUD element a background, border, rounded corners, padding, opacity, text size and line spacing, from the on-screen editor or by writing theme.cfg (next to hud.cfg, in a short CSS-like format). It changes live in a running game. With no theme the HUD looks exactly as before. In ranked, a theme can dim an element to 30% at least, never hide it.
  • The score box, kill feed and chat are now HUD elements: move, size, recolour and style them like the others (Settings > HUD and EDIT ON SCREEN).
  • Fixed the HUD tab's footer covering the weapon list: BACK and RESET WHOLE HUD now sit higher, clear of the HUD's bottom row.
  • The HUD and menus draw a little cheaper: the HUD costs slightly less CPU per frame than before even with all of the above, and every piece of text and every box in the UI sends a third less data to the GPU.

Video

  • RENDER SCALE (Settings > VIDEO + AUDIO > IMAGE, 50% to 200%, default 100%): draws the 3D world at that share of your window's resolution and stretches it to fit, while the HUD, text and menus stay at full resolution and sharp. Below 100% for more frame rate on a weak GPU; above 100% for a smoother, supersampled picture. Works with every ANTI-ALIASING mode. F3 shows the resolution the world is drawn at (3D 960X540). At 100% nothing changes.
  • SHARPEN (next to it, on by default): below 100%, sharpens the stretched world a little so it looks less blurry. It does nothing at 100% and above, or with ANTI-ALIASING: SMOOTH, which smooths the edges instead.
  • BRIGHTNESS and PLAYER SHADOWS now share a row, to make room.

Benchmark

  • BENCHMARK on the main menu: RUN BENCHMARK plays a Hamlet free for all demo (shipped with the game) with the HUD off and a small BENCHMARK / time left box; Esc cancels. The first 3 s aren't counted. Then a result page: a frame-time graph of the whole run with lines at 60 / 144 / 240 fps, AVG FPS, 1% LOW, 0.1% LOW, MIN FPS (the worst frame), STDEV (ms), the frame count, and your setup (resolution, render scale, display, vsync, FPS limit, AA, shadows, CPU, GPU, driver, version), next to the run before it.
  • Runs are saved like demos in benchmarks/ in your game data folder, one file each. The BENCHMARKS page lists them; open any to see its result page, give it a NAME (it starts as the build's label, the map and the date), DELETE it, or EXPORT CSV (every frame's time in whole microseconds, one a line) or EXPORT PNG (a picture of the result page) into benchmarks/exports/; OPEN FOLDER opens it. A FLAGS.txt next to the game's exe names the runs of that build (its first line).

  • Dash tech: jump during a dash on the floor to keep its full speed (dash jump); look down and dash into the floor holding jump for even more speed and a low hop (super dash, up to 36 m/s); dash into a wall head-on (under 45°) to bounce off it at full dash speed.

  • Rockets fly straight (no drop; was 12 m/s²). The alt-fire lob drops at 13.5 m/s² (was 36), so it arcs gently instead of falling at your feet. Stickies no longer inherit your sideways speed, only forward / back and up / down, so strafing doesn't throw them off target.
  • Faster movement: run 9.5 m/s (was 8), ladders 6.75 m/s up and 4.5 sideways (were 4.5 / 3), and you can steer a standing jump. Ground acceleration is 16 (was 12): full speed about as soon as before at the higher top speed.

  • New sounds: a super (dash jump), a hyper (super dash) and the locator each have their own.
  • Thrown knives, crossbow bolts and thrown syringes stay where they land: in the wall until the match ends, or in the player they hit until that player dies. Just for show: nothing about the weapons changed.
  • Dashes keep 1 m/s of each dash (was 3).
  • Two dash charges, on top of the dash cooldown: a dash needs a charge and the 1.2 s cooldown, which starts when your last dash ends. Landing refills both charges, a bunny hop refills one, a dash jump leaves both, and a super dash or a wall bounce leaves one. You can't dash during a dash. The HUD's dash pips fill up while the cooldown runs.
  • Health bars are switched on for everyone once with this update (now the default); turn them off again in SETTINGS if you prefer.

Bots, rebuilt

  • They only know what a player could: a view cone with line of sight, hearing gunfire and footsteps with a rough direction, and memory of where they last saw you. No more 360° vision.
  • They react like people (450 / 300 / 200 / 150 ms by level), flick and overshoot, lose track of fast strafers and misjudge leads. Levels are clearly apart; experts are strong but beatable.
  • Difficulty no longer depends on the server tick rate. Advanced and expert bots bunny-hop, air-strafe, dash and grapple, and no longer dash into pits.
  • Bots keep off the edges: they simulate a grapple pull before taking it, never hop or dash toward a drop, and fight away from ledges (falls over every map in testing: 56 -> 5); fewer self-kills with rockets and stickies. They use their real (size-modified) hitbox for objectives, and hide and seek / tag bots no longer pick spots from the previous map. A bot added during a tag match plays the next one.
  • Navigation is built in the background at map load (no hitch when the first bot spawns). Fixed bots that sometimes never fired after switching weapons.

0.36.0

Fixes

  • Knife throw: right-clicking to throw the knife no longer fires the weapon you switch to (the rocket lob) straight away. Let go of the button, then click again.
  • Rocket alt-fire: the lob now flies 1.4x as fast (70% of rocket speed), with the same 3x gravity.

Server: less CPU, less bandwidth

    • Your input is still simulated every millisecond.
    • Interpolation of other players grows by about 2 ms (7.1 → 9.2 ms on the localhost test).

    • Projectile velocity is only sent when it changes.
    • Reliable messages are re-sent once per round trip instead of in every snapshot.
    • Result: 172 → 129 bytes per snapshot. With half as many snapshots, upload per player is about 62% lower (≈1.4 → 0.5 Mbit/s).
  • Snapshot buffers are reused instead of being reallocated every send.
  • An empty server sleeps between ticks instead of busy-waiting.
  • Benchmark (quark_tests serverbench): Harbor, 12 bots + 4 players, 102 → 69 µs per tick at 1000 Hz.

Protocol

  • Network protocol version 19: everyone needs 0.36.0.

0.35.0

Faster frames

  • World culling: the world is split into 12 m chunks, and only the ones in view are drawn, in a single draw call. Before, every surface was drawn every frame, including everything behind you.
  • World shader: same image (identical to within 1/255 per pixel), less work per pixel. Map colours are converted once per frame instead of once per pixel, and glow trim and distant scenery skip the lighting entirely.
  • Players, projectiles and effects outside the view are skipped. Their shadows still show up.
  • F3 stats no longer sort every frame time twice a second; that could itself cause the spikes it measures.
  • Frame cap: no longer drifts slightly below the rate you set.
  • Other: shader uniform lookups are a hash instead of a linear search, and finished sounds leave the audio mixer without shuffling the voice list.
  • Benchmark (software rendering): Foundry 35.6 → 48.2 FPS average, 1% low 21.0 → 29.3. Harbor 44.8 → 54.0 FPS, 1% low 31.5 → 42.5.

Anti-aliasing (Settings, right column; off by default)

  • SMOOTH EDGES (CHEAP): one screen-space pass that smooths only high-contrast edges, which in quark means the outlines. Everything else passes through untouched.
  • MSAA 2X / 4X: multisampled 3D scene. The HUD and menus are drawn after the resolve, so they don't pay for it.

Tools

  • quark --bench SECONDS: plays a vs-bots match with a slow look around, then prints avg / 1% low / 0.1% low FPS, the worst frame, and the CPU part of each frame.
  • Profiling builds (-DQUARK_ZONESTATS=ON) print per-zone times, including each zone's worst frame.
  • --menushot captures menu screens headless.
  • quark.cfg (your settings) is no longer tracked in the repository.

0.33.0

Hide and seek (new mode)

  • One player seeks, everyone else hides. Movement is run and jump only: no dash, no grapple.
  • For the first 30 seconds the seeker faces the wall and counts on a blacked-out screen while the hiders hide.
  • The seeker has the knife, and one stab finds a hider. Nobody else has a weapon, and the knife can't be thrown.
  • The seeker wins the round by finding everyone (+3). If anyone stays hidden for 5 minutes, the hiders win (+2 each).
  • Everyone takes a turn seeking, and the top score wins the match.

Tag (new mode)

  • Like hide and seek, but tagging someone with the knife makes them "it". They stand still for 2 seconds, so there's no instant tag-back.
  • Each round lasts 1 minute. Whoever is "it" when time runs out is eliminated, and the next round starts with a new random "it".
  • The last player left wins.
  • An IT marker floats over whoever is "it". Each player sees a banner with their role.

A long course over a chasm. It starts on a bridge on stone arches, then goes:

  • through a covered lane and up a climb,

0.19.0

Netcode

  • Delta snapshots. The server only sends what changed since the last snapshot your client confirmed receiving. An unchanged player costs 3 bytes and an unchanged mode state 1 byte; mode timers are sent in tenths of a second, so they rarely change. If that snapshot is too old, a full one is sent instead, so packet loss can't break it.
    • With 8 players all moving, snapshots dropped from about 420 to about 180 bytes; when players are standing still they're much smaller still.
    • That's what lets big servers keep higher tick rates.
  • Adaptive interpolation delay. Other players used to be drawn a fixed 3 snapshots + 10 ms behind (13 ms at 1000 Hz). The client now measures how late snapshots actually arrive (99th percentile) and uses 2 snapshots plus that, between 1 and 50 ms. On a clean connection enemies are drawn several milliseconds closer to where they really are, and the server rewinds less for hit checks.
  • Enemies out of sight aren't sent. The server checks line of sight 20 times a second. Enemies you can't see arrive without a position, so wallhacks get nothing. Their name and score still reach the scoreboard.
    • Anyone within 20 m (earshot), teammates, and everyone while you're dead or spectating are always sent.
    • A player stays visible for 0.3 s after you lose sight of them, and their movement is extrapolated slightly, so peeks never pop in late.

0.3.0

Movement

  • Left Shift: dash in the direction you're moving (or forward), 1.2 s cooldown. Keeps your speed if you're already faster.
  • Hold Space: automatic bunny hopping. You jump the moment you land, before ground friction slows you down.
  • Q: momentum grapple (like TF2). Press to fire at what you're aiming at (45 m range), hold to be pulled in. The pull adds to your momentum instead of replacing it, so you can swing. Everyone can see your rope.

Server tick rate

  • The server now starts at 1000 Hz and sends a snapshot every tick.
  • During warmup it checks whether it can keep up: late ticks, failed sends, and snapshot loss reported by every player. If not, it steps down to 500, then 250, then 125 Hz (the minimum) and tells everyone.
  • quark_server --tickrate N sets the highest rate to try.
  • Movement is simulated in fixed 1 ms steps, so it is exactly the same at every tick rate and every frame rate (checked by an automated test: identical positions at 1000/500/250/125 Hz with dashing, bhopping and grappling).
  • Other players are drawn 3 snapshots + 10 ms behind: 13 ms at 1000 Hz (was 50 ms).
  • Client prediction skips its replay when the server agrees with what was predicted (almost always), so high tick rates cost very little CPU.
  • Windows: 1 ms timer precision for accurate ticks; the hosting thread runs at above-normal priority.

Other

  • Servers are limited to 8 players (keeps 1000 Hz affordable on home connections).
  • Protocol version 3: older clients and servers can't connect to this version.
