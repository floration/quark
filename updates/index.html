1.17.0Latest

A big one: two security reviews fixed, the owner's design decisions applied, rebuilt bots, a new font, and a new release process. Network protocol 42: old and new versions can't play together, so update servers and games together.

Gameplay

  • Overtime in payload, neutral payload, multi point and bomb: if the side that needs time is on the objective (or touched it in the last 5 s) when the clock runs out, the round goes on until 5 s after their last touch. The HUD shows OVERTIME.
  • Headshots are the head only: shoulders count as body. The sniper does a flat 50.
  • Headshot damage numbers are red.
  • Fixed spread patterns: the shotgun always fires the same pellet layout, and weapons with spread follow a fixed sequence while you hold fire. Your tracers show exactly where the pellets go.
  • Players with a high interpolation setting (up to 200 ms) now hit what they see.
  • The syringe is only in modes where you respawn (not VS, bomb, duel, 1v1 or tourney), unless a custom mode lists it.
  • Knife throw: the knife is disabled for 4 s (greyed with a timer), and nothing can be used for the knife's normal swing cooldown (0.75 s). The old shared throw cooldown and the extra delay after a throw are gone.
  • Syringe: each use heals you 10 HP, with a 1 s cooldown (was 1 HP every 0.1 s). A throw heals a teammate, disables your syringe for 4 s, and nothing can be used for 1 s. A teammate's syringe hit also brings back your thrown knife or syringe at once.
  • Weapon balance: rifle 4.95 a body shot (was 5.95) and 7.5 a headshot (was 8.5), no spread, and scoped it fires only 10% slower (was 25%); the shotgun's knockback is doubled; the laser reaches 24 m (was 32): more damage up close, the rifle wins at range; the sniper does a flat 50.
  • Projectiles fly in fixed 1 ms steps, so rockets, stickies and bolts follow the same arc on every server tick rate, and your screen draws them along that arc, gravity included.
  • The grapple no longer slows you down when it catches.
  • The grapple sticks to enemies: a hook that hits an enemy deals 10 damage (15 to the head) and pulls you both together until you meet (release, a dash, or your own grapple breaks it; teammates are passed through; in instagib, hide and seek and tag it only pulls).
  • Stickies stick to enemies they hit and ride along on them until you set them off, for the full blast (they see and hear STICKY ON YOU).
  • Dash tech: jump during a dash on the floor to keep its full speed (dash jump); look down and dash into the floor holding jump for even more speed and a low hop (super dash, up to 36 m/s); dash into a wall head-on (under 45°) to bounce off it at full dash speed.
  • Size modifiers (double size, giant, tiny) scale your collision box, not just your hitbox and camera.
  • A giant never spawns inside a wall: if no spawn fits, it spawns crouched or just beside one.
  • Rockets fly straight (no drop; was 12 m/s²). The alt-fire lob drops at 13.5 m/s² (was 36), so it arcs gently instead of falling at your feet. Stickies no longer inherit your sideways speed, only forward / back and up / down, so strafing doesn't throw them off target.
  • Faster movement: run 9.5 m/s (was 8), ladders 6.75 m/s up and 4.5 sideways (were 4.5 / 3), and you can steer a standing jump. Ground acceleration is 16 (was 12): full speed about as soon as before at the higher top speed.
  • At speed, A/D strafing turns better than holding W, from 9.5 m/s (run speed) up: holding W alone steers less and less above that.
  • Holding jump refills only one dash charge: bunny hopping never builds the second one back; land without jump held for both.
  • New sounds: a super (dash jump), a hyper (super dash) and the locator each have their own.
  • Thrown knives, crossbow bolts and thrown syringes stay where they land: in the wall until the match ends, or in the player they hit until that player dies. Just for show: nothing about the weapons changed.
  • Dashes keep 1 m/s of each dash (was 3).
  • Two dash charges, on top of the dash cooldown: a dash needs a charge and the 1.2 s cooldown, which starts when your last dash ends. Landing refills both charges, a bunny hop refills one, a dash jump leaves both, and a super dash or a wall bounce leaves one. You can't dash during a dash. The HUD's dash pips fill up while the cooldown runs.
  • Health bars are switched on for everyone once with this update (now the default); turn them off again in SETTINGS if you prefer.
  • Type a number into any slider, and right-click a slider to reset it to its default (including the custom-mode MOVEMENT page).
  • New settings: damage-number size, the damage flash at the screen's edges (now off by default; turn it on or set its strength in VISUALS), zoom FOV for the sniper's and rifle's alt-fire, and the map colour for fullbright flat textures.
  • Map editor: the grid goes down to 1/8 m, and the grid button says 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2 and 4 m.
  • The knife's slash comes out of the knife you hold, like any weapon's shot, and has no box at its end any more.
  • Every button in the game is now tested automatically, which fixed buttons that did nothing, settings that didn't stick, wrong Esc behaviour and cut-off text.
  • Name colour: pick the colour others see your name in on the scoreboard, in chat and in the kill feed (SETTINGS). Name tags over teammates keep the team colour.
  • Hide and seek scoring: everyone seeks once. A hider still unfound when the round ends scores 1, and the seeker scores 1 for each hider found; a found hider scores nothing, and a fall scores nobody anything.
  • Hide and seek tiebreak: players tied for first after the last round play rock paper scissors for the match. Between two it's best of 3 (a draw is thrown again); with three or more, everyone throws at once and the losing shape is out (one shape, or all three: throw again) until two remain. Pick with 1 / 2 / 3 within 6 s; no pick means a random one.
  • The locator is a weapon (key 0) with its own model, held only by the seeker and the tagger: fire it to see one random hider / runner through walls for 1 s, every 5 s. It exists only in hide and seek and tag (other modes and custom modes never get it), and the use key no longer triggers it. Bot seekers and taggers use it too.
  • Hide and seek and tag: the seeker / tagger is seen holding the knife (or locator); hiders and runners hold nothing. Hiders are no longer shown the seeker's position through walls.
  • Tag: runners always see where the tagger is: a marker through walls with the distance, or an arrow at the screen edge.
  • Tag: going to spectator or leaving during a match eliminates you, and you stay out if you reconnect.
  • Bomb: a dropped bomb falls to the floor and goes back to an attacker after 15 s.
  • Heals stop at your life's starting health. Balance bots aren't removed mid-round while alive. The FFA scoreboard shows frags. Colosseum's tourney uses its opposite-end spawns. The server tick rate only changes between rounds.
  • Fixed: the laser did ~25x damage on servers that had been up for hours (the server clock lost millisecond precision). The server clock and every timer now use full precision and never wrap, however long a server runs.

Bots, rebuilt

  • They only know what a player could: a view cone with line of sight, hearing gunfire and footsteps with a rough direction, and memory of where they last saw you. No more 360° vision.
  • They react like people (450 / 300 / 200 / 150 ms by level), flick and overshoot, lose track of fast strafers and misjudge leads. Levels are clearly apart; experts are strong but beatable.
  • Difficulty no longer depends on the server tick rate. Advanced and expert bots bunny-hop, air-strafe, dash and grapple, and no longer dash into pits.
  • Bots keep off the edges: they simulate a grapple pull before taking it, never hop or dash toward a drop, and fight away from ledges (falls over every map in testing: 56 -> 5); fewer self-kills with rockets and stickies. They use their real (size-modified) hitbox for objectives, and hide and seek / tag bots no longer pick spots from the previous map. A bot added during a tag match plays the next one.
  • Navigation is built in the background at map load (no hitch when the first bot spawns). Fixed bots that sometimes never fired after switching weapons.

Ranked

  • Your ranked id is proven by your key, so nobody can play under your id, not even a server you join that passes your connection on to another one: what your game signs names the server you joined. (A dedicated server that players reach at an address it can't see itself, such as a router forwarding a different port or a container, says so with --own-addr host:port.) Your ratings, race times and Discord link carry over automatically the first time you start this version (the game keeps asking until the ranking server confirms it, and a match rated before that is merged in rather than replacing your old rating).
  • Match tickets: a ranked match only counts if the server opened it with the ranking server and the players' results agree. If your game sees a different result than the host's, it reports a dispute instead, and a disputed match is never rated (it's logged).
  • Live ranked matches are locked (1v1 vanilla, 1v1 laser vampire and FFA instagib; the Viaduct race isn't): no votes, map or mode changes, ranked off, kicks or team switches until it's over.
  • The first player to leave a live ranked match loses it; the player who stayed can then leave freely.
  • Race times are reported by the server that saw the run, and impossible splits are refused.
  • Discord links are verified: run /link in Discord, type the code in SETTINGS > DISCORD CODE. Crown roles go only to verified links. Linking shows what becomes public.
  • New accounts are provisional for 5 matches (one placement threshold everywhere).
  • A reconnect gets its old slot back only if it proves the same ranked id, and an FFA leaver who rejoins is counted once, as the leaver.
  • Spectators of a ranked match see health bars, crowns, models and HUD extras again; only the players have them hidden.

Servers and connections

  • 10 s reconnect grace in every game: your slot, team and score are kept; in a tourney a drop is no longer an instant walkover.
  • Tourneys are seeded by your average rating across the three ranked ladders (200 if unrated); top seeds get the byes.
  • Hosting shows if your port is reachable: the pause menu shows the UPnP result and whether friends can actually reach you (green / red with advice), with CHECK AGAIN.
  • Admin is the player hosting the game, nobody else. kick takes a player's name (or id), and addbot takes a skill name.
  • Joining does a quick address check, so forged packets can't make a server flood someone or kick a player; server-list queries and version-mismatch replies can't be used for amplification; hit rewind is capped by your real ping; chat is filtered and rate-limited; votes check map names and have a cooldown.
  • A malicious server or map can no longer write files outside the game folder, crash the game or freeze it; settings from a server that aren't real numbers fall back to defaults. A broken demo, package or server reply fails with an error, never a crash.
  • Hidden information: your game is no longer sent the health, weapon or firing of players you can't see, nor projectiles out of your sight (wallhacks have less to show). In hide and seek the seeker gets nothing about the hiders during the head start. While you're dead you see what the player you watch sees, and explosions out of sight still sound where they happen. Spectators still see everything.
  • New vote: move a player to spectator (callvote spec <name>, from 3 players): for someone AFK or causing trouble. They stay out of play until the next map. Kicking stays with the host or the server console and can't be voted.
  • The ranking server's address is built into the game (only a file signed with the release key can move it); it's hardened against spam, with cached leaderboards.
  • Smoother servers with many players: line-of-sight checks are spread over the ticks and capped (Midtown with 32 players: worst tick 11-13 ms -> under 1 ms), and sending snapshots allocates no memory.
  • Hosted edit sessions no longer hitch every 5 s: the autosave runs off the game thread.
  • Stopping or leaving a hosted game no longer freezes the screen for up to 15 s.
  • --own-addr is explained in the README, and a game hosted from the menu says on the pause screen when players reach it at an address it can't confirm as its own.
  • Fixed: a custom mode whose map list started with a blank entry switched the host to a map with no name.
  • Every server box runs a production and a testing server ("[TESTING]", next port up), each on the latest build of its channel. The testing server only runs while there's a testing build newer than production; otherwise it's off. A load governor shares the machine: when it can't carry both, the empty server slows first, then the one not playing a ranked match, then the other, down to 125 Hz; if that isn't enough the testing server closes until things calm down. Production never closes.
  • Ranked on testing servers and testing builds is played but never rated ("TESTING SERVER - RANKED NOT RATED"), and the ranking server refuses their results.

Your files and updates

  • Old demos are converted when you update (1.11 and later), so they keep playing; the originals are kept in demos/old/<version>/, and one that can't be converted says which version recorded it.
  • Updates delete only retired game files (ones an earlier version installed that this one doesn't): never your settings, maps, demos, screenshots or anything you made.
  • Your data lives in your user folder (Windows %APPDATA%\quark, Linux ~/.local/share/quark, macOS ~/Library/Application Support/quark): settings, ranked key, demos, HUDs, your maps. It's copied over on first start, and uninstalling never deletes it.
  • Folder names with non-ASCII characters (such as an accented Windows user name) work for every file the game reads and writes.
  • Replacement assets: sounds and voices (.wav, .ogg, .flac, .mp3), glTF models, the font and the icon can be replaced by files in overrides/ in your user folder (see docs/ASSETS.md).
  • Everything published is signed: game packages, test builds, the launcher (the standalone downloads too) and the website's file list. The launcher refuses any update whose signature doesn't check out, and checks that the signed release list names this exact version, channel and file. Versions from before signing (1.16 and older) install like any other, with no warning.
  • If an antivirus quarantines a game file (it happened to quark.exe of 1.15.1 and 1.16.0 on one PC: a false positive on an unsigned build), the launcher now notices: an update stops before anything changes (RETRY), and the game is never started without its exe. You're told how to restore it or add an exclusion, and REPAIR reinstalls your version; CHECK FILES checks every game file.
  • Windows exes now carry version information and a manifest (antivirus scanners flag anonymous exes more), and on Windows an update keeps your old quark.cfg and replaces an older installed launcher properly.
  • Launcher: a CHANNEL button switches between PRODUCTION and TESTING builds and lets you pick any version. It never downgrades on its own, updates itself, and the game brings every older launcher (and its shortcuts) up to date, so you never need to download the launcher again.

Interface

  • New font: Work Sans Bold, sharp at every size, with real lowercase.
  • Console: monospaced; Tab no longer opens the scoreboard; Tab lists every completion, for every command and argument. Chat and console have a real caret (arrows, Home / End, Delete).
  • Alt+Enter toggles borderless fullscreen and windowed; holding it no longer also presses Enter.
  • Chat: a Toggle chat key (unbound by default) hides or shows incoming chat, and SETTINGS has a Disable chat option that stays in sync with it. A chat key bound to a letter no longer types that letter into the chat.
  • Hitsounds come in four tiers by damage (under 10, 10-24, 25-50, over 50), each heavier and louder, in every sound pack (and replaceable in the Asset Studio: HIT, HIT_MED, HIT_HEAVY, HIT_BRUTAL).
  • Crosshair hit flash: choose its colour, or turn it off (the default is unchanged).
  • Health bars over other players are on by default for new players (still never in ranked; SETTINGS turns them off).
  • Sniper scope off: with SCOPE OVERLAY at 0 the sniper zooms with your normal crosshair, like the rifle and crossbow, instead of no crosshair at all.
  • Outline thickness: separate settings for the world's outlines and for players' outlines.
  • Map lighting is worked out in the background with a loading bar: the window no longer says "Not Responding", and joining a big map on a remote server no longer times out. It also fits your graphics card's largest texture size.
  • Fewer hitches: rescanning maps, refreshing the server list (it could freeze for 6 s while a fetch was pending), recording a demo and changing the announcer voice no longer stall the game.
  • Text that doesn't fit shrinks, then clips with "..." instead of overflowing. Long names no longer run into the next column in the scoreboard, the server list or the Discord setting.

Editor

  • Editing a built-in map: when you save or leave, it asks whether to keep your edits as a new map of your own (e.g. temple_edit). Built-in maps are never changed, and hosted edit sessions on them are never saved over them.
  • Fixed the crash when opening a map ("mymap"), and maps saving as "mymap" whatever you typed (SAVE AS in shared sessions).
  • Trim and clip (invisible wall) boxes can be edited, deleted and undone; the material cycle includes TRIM and CLIP, and clip boxes show see-through violet.
  • Move and resize handles on all six faces, visible and grabbable through other objects.
  • Edits apply in place (big group edits sync 4-5x faster); maps load about 4-6x faster.
  • Clip boxes can be selected from anywhere: click the same spot again to pick the next thing along your view, and FIND CLIP BOXES steps through every invisible wall. When handles overlap, the nearest one is grabbed.
  • An edit with several parts applies whole or not at all, undo included. Saved maps keep every digit an edit carries, so an edited map reloads exactly as edited.
  • A map that can't be saved under its own name gets a free name (mymap2, NAME_new2) and never overwrites another map.
  • Faster on big maps: the map is drawn as one static batch (about a third less frame time on Midtown), and there's no full rebuild after each edit.

Maps

  • Maps are generated from tools/make_maps.py (one file per map in tools/maps/) when the game is built. The map report grades maps against what movement can really do (bunny-hops, strafing, dash-ups, grapple) and checks nobody can get out of bounds.
  • Dojo: an invisible wall at the moat's far edge (a running jump used to clear it).
  • Maps are identified by the SHA-256 of their content (ranked map list, lighting caches, map downloads, editing sessions).
  • Map files have a version line, and every change to the format comes with a converter: older maps are converted when they load, and your own maps are converted once at startup (the originals are kept in maps/old/v<N>/). A map from a newer version still loads, skipping what this one doesn't know. Staircases and the map's setting are real keywords, kept by the editor.
  • Hand-made maps are checked and scored like generated ones but don't have to pass the automated map checks; their scores are listed in the map report for balancing.
  • Every offered mode has enough spawns on every map, spawns face open space and are spread out, ranked maps are checked for point symmetry, and the payload cart's path is checked against the geometry.
  • Ranked maps (Anvil, Dojo, Furnace, Gantry, Temple) have the sun straight overhead, so both sides get the same light.
  • Team spawns can no longer see the enemy's spawns: screens on Canal and Railyard, a screen wall before the defenders' north-yard spawns on Depot, bus shelters on Downtown, cover on Lodestone, moved pads on Midtown.
  • Railyard: a box on the cart's track moved beside it. Lodestone: the cart runs beside the station landing. Viaduct: a pillar no longer blocks the second race leg, and the start grid holds 8 racers.
  • Attackers and defenders reach every bomb site and point at about the same time (within 1 s, graded at the worst one): defenders used to get there up to 13 s early. Midtown and Lodestone's multi point spawns are re-staged; Depot's bomb sites are now halfway down each side (among the warehouse racks and on the loading dock); Lodestone's bomb sites moved to the middle of the mine (attackers used to reach one 10.8 s first).
  • Stricter map grading: only floor you can reach counts, loops and height are measured for real, balance is checked for every objective of every mode, and the long sightline band follows the laser's 24 m. Maps that fell below 7 were fixed: more cover on Midtown and Lodestone, extra ladders to the roof walks on Colosseum, Furnace and Temple, head-high field walls on Hamlet (and ways out of its creek, a ladder each side of the water tower), lights on Harbor, crowd barriers on Viaduct, more clutter and ladders on Depot, Railyard and Foundry.
  • Lodestone is graded in every mode it offers: its race now ends on the cage landing so it can't be skipped, free for all has 12 spread-out spawns, and the ore-cart track has cover along both sides. Lodestone is no longer offered for team VS, duel, 1v1 or tourney: those want a small arena, and the mine is about 30 times too big (you can still start it there, with a warning).

Website

  • New Assets and Sounds pages: spin every model and map in 3D and play every sound and announcer line, for the production or testing build, then make your own model (.glb) or sound (.wav) and drop it into your overrides/ folder.

Release process

  • New work lands on a testing branch as signed prereleases (the launcher's TESTING channel, and #testing-updates on Discord). Production releases come only from a version tag on the protected main branch and need Windows, Linux and macOS.
  • Tests run on Windows in CI too, and under address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers with fuzzing of packets, maps, custom modes and packages. Dedicated servers update only to signed releases of their own channel (production or testing) and run as their own sandboxed user.
  • Discord bot: at most 5 live server channels, a Testing Updates role, secrets in a protected file. /changelog understands testing builds, and the bot uses the game's built-in ranking server address.

1.12.2

Smoother servers under load

  • A busy server falling behind used to skip ahead in time, which made its clock jump.
    • Players' games read those jumps as network jitter and buffered more, so everyone else was drawn further in the past (31-54 ms instead of about 10 ms on a loaded 2-core machine).

0.35.0

Faster frames

  • World culling: the world is split into 12 m chunks, and only the ones in view are drawn, in a single draw call. Before, every surface was drawn every frame, including everything behind you.
  • World shader: same image (identical to within 1/255 per pixel), less work per pixel. Map colours are converted once per frame instead of once per pixel, and glow trim and distant scenery skip the lighting entirely.

  • SMOOTH EDGES (CHEAP): one screen-space pass that smooths only high-contrast edges, which in quark means the outlines. Everything else passes through untouched.

0.19.0

Netcode

  • Delta snapshots. The server only sends what changed since the last snapshot your client confirmed receiving. An unchanged player costs 3 bytes and an unchanged mode state 1 byte; mode timers are sent in tenths of a second, so they rarely change. If that snapshot is too old, a full one is sent instead, so packet loss can't break it.
    • With 8 players all moving, snapshots dropped from about 420 to about 180 bytes; when players are standing still they're much smaller still.
    • That's what lets big servers keep higher tick rates.
  • Adaptive interpolation delay. Other players used to be drawn a fixed 3 snapshots + 10 ms behind (13 ms at 1000 Hz). The client now measures how late snapshots actually arrive (99th percentile) and uses 2 snapshots plus that, between 1 and 50 ms. On a clean connection enemies are drawn several milliseconds closer to where they really are, and the server rewinds less for hit checks.
  • Enemies out of sight aren't sent. The server checks line of sight 20 times a second. Enemies you can't see arrive without a position, so wallhacks get nothing. Their name and score still reach the scoreboard.
    • Anyone within 20 m (earshot), teammates, and everyone while you're dead or spectating are always sent.
    • A player stays visible for 0.3 s after you lose sight of them, and their movement is extrapolated slightly, so peeks never pop in late.

0.18.0

Performance (measured with Tracy, 20 s of a bot match on Harbor)

  • Text draws once per character instead of 9 times. The 1-pixel black outline is now done in the shader, and UI vertices are half the size (16 bytes). Vertex data uploaded per frame went from about 400 KB to about 85 KB, and 2D drawing from 0.72 ms to 0.15 ms (median).
  • No first-frame hitch. Every shader and blend state is compiled while the map loads, behind the loading screen, instead of on the first frame of the match. The first in-game frame went from 57 ms to 6 ms, and the worst frame after it from 24 ms to 11 ms.
  • Less shadow work. Player and projectile shadows are only sampled inside the area those objects can actually shade. Everywhere else, the world and player shaders skip the 4 shadow samples per pixel. This helps most on integrated GPUs and at high resolutions.

  • Matches are now best of 5 (first to 3).

Movement

  • Left Shift: dash in the direction you're moving (or forward), 1.2 s cooldown. Keeps your speed if you're already faster.
  • Hold Space: automatic bunny hopping. You jump the moment you land, before ground friction slows you down.

Server tick rate

  • The server now starts at 1000 Hz and sends a snapshot every tick.
  • During warmup it checks whether it can keep up: late ticks, failed sends, and snapshot loss reported by every player. If not, it steps down to 500, then 250, then 125 Hz (the minimum) and tells everyone.
  • quark_server --tickrate N sets the highest rate to try.
  • Movement is simulated in fixed 1 ms steps, so it is exactly the same at every tick rate and every frame rate (checked by an automated test: identical positions at 1000/500/250/125 Hz with dashing, bhopping and grappling).
  • Other players are drawn 3 snapshots + 10 ms behind: 13 ms at 1000 Hz (was 50 ms).
  • Client prediction skips its replay when the server agrees with what was predicted (almost always), so high tick rates cost very little CPU.
  • Windows: 1 ms timer precision for accurate ticks; the hosting thread runs at above-normal priority.
