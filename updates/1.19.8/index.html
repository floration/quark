quark 1.19.8

Everything that changed in this version, and why.

Every change since production 1.19.7, with what changed and why. Human-made assets: 16.0% (tools/provenance.py summary). Protocol 69 (round hitboxes; 1.19.7 is 68), map format 14, unchanged from 1.19.7.

Hit registration (owner, 2026-11-04: "why are the hitboxes cubes and not capsules")

  • Hits test two upright cylinders with flat ends instead of two boxes (hitShape, rayCylinderY in pmove.h; protocol 69). The two axis-aligned boxes never turned with the player, so a standing player was 0.8 m wide face-on but 1.13 m across the corners (+41%) from 45 degrees, and the corners stuck out past the model. Now:
    • The body is a cylinder the hull's full width (radius 0.4 m standing) from the feet up to the head's bottom; the head is a head-wide cylinder (radius 0.18 m standing, kHeadWidthFraction) from there to the top. Flat ends on both (the owner: "the bottom can be flat too, this makes calc easier, same with head it can be a cylinder"; a capsule body and head sphere were the first version of this change). Seen from the front they are exactly the old boxes, so the owner's 2026-10-27 rule holds unchanged: nothing beside the head over the shoulders, just under the head's bottom is a body hit.
    • Size modifiers (giant, tiny) and the knife's wider swing (grow) scale both, as before.
    • The movement hull is unchanged (a box), and so is the network traffic.
  • Cost: the same as before. A shot first tests the old box around both (the cheap reject). The side's hit-or-miss is the line's squared distance from the axis against the radius squared, and a flat end is a plane and a squared distance; the one square root (the exact entry on the side) is taken only when the line passes within the radius.
  • Everything that tests a player uses it: hitscan on the server (with lag compensation), projectiles, glass shards, the knife, the grapple's catch (pmove: the client's prediction too), the client's own hit reports and sparks, and blasts (hitboxClosest: the nearest point on the body or the head). quark_maptool's pellet stats (wpn_stats.cpp) see the same rectangles from the front as before.
  • Tests (rules head): every size still passes the head, beside-the-head, shoulder-corner, under-the-chin, from-above and knife checks, plus a new one: a level shot 2% inside the radius hits and 2% outside misses from 0, 23, 45 and 69 degrees (a box failed this at 45).

Build and release (CI)

  • The macOS build goes to an Intel Mac first, with arm64 as the fallback. On 2026-11-02, from 13:39 to 23:08 UTC, GitHub's macOS arm64 pool was full. Every macos-latest job sat queued about 15 minutes, then ended with "The job was not acquired by Runner of type hosted even after multiple attempts". That turned every macos run red (runs 187-205), and those testing builds and releases 1.19.5 and 1.19.6 went out without macOS files. A probe at 22:55 UTC showed the split: macos-14 got a runner in 3 s, macos-15-intel in 19 s, macos-latest and macos-26 only after 13 min, and macos-15 never did. The build steps moved to .github/actions/macos-build. The macos job now runs them on macos-15-intel, and the new macos-arm job (macos-latest) runs them only when the Intel job didn't build. Both make the same universal (arm64 + x86_64, macOS 10.15+) files, and publish takes whichever one built. The trade-off: when no Intel Mac picks the job up, the arm64 fallback starts about 15 min later and the run still shows that first job as failed. GitHub plans to retire macos-15-intel in August 2027; switch the order back before then.

Tests (quark_tests)

  • modelockstep2: no more "winner at N Hz: no hooks". The lockstep cases still had a winner stays case. Winner stays was retired (1v1 rotates the line itself now), so a server started in it plays 1v1 (retiredTo). Logic compiled for winner stays never hooks into a 1v1 server, which made it fail at 1000, 250 and 125 Hz, on 1.19.6 too. The case is gone, and 1v1's case covers the line.
  • The 3D flying checks (rules flymod, vamp3d) no longer fail under load. pump() counts wall-clock time, and under load the server's own clock falls behind it, so 0.5 s after addBot the flymod bot often hadn't spawned yet. With eight busy cores beside it, the check failed in 8 of 8 runs; now it fails in 0 of 8. Both tests now pump server time and wait (for up to 5 s of game time) for the spawn and for the fly flag's snapshot.
  • The movement group's air drift checks follow kAirDriftAccel. The owner set air drift to 0 on 2026-11-01 (protocol 63). The checks still expected 8 m/s² of drift: a standing jump + W at about 7 m/s, a long fall reaching 9.5 m/s, and a sideways push past 3 m/s. That made 3 checks fail. With drift at 0, they now check the strafe push alone: 2.30 m/s for W-only and 2.53 m/s for A/D. If drift comes back, the old numbers apply again.

Performance (owner, 2026-11-02: "checks the game for performance blocks"; desktop Tracy profile 2026-11-03: GPU-bound, the GPU world pass is 176 of ~290 µs a frame)

  • Cobblestone (material 24) shader (1.20 list C, owner 2026-11-03 "go ahead and do all of this"; q's bench 2026-10-29: an all-cobblestone map's 0.1% low was ~800 FPS against ~2800 with the old materials). Per pixel the look paid for an exact border-distance Voronoi over 3x3 + 5x5 cells (34 hash22 = 68 sin) plus a 3-octave fbm (12 hash2): about 80 sin calls. Now:
    • the border pass looks at the 3x3 cells around the nearest one, not 5x5 (9 instead of 25). It can miss a bisector two cells out only when a point sits in a far corner, which moves a joint by well under a pixel;
    • far / grazing (a stone under ~2.5 pixels), the cells are skipped altogether: the stones' average colour, faded in over the same footprint PATTERN FADE uses, so nothing pops;
    • the grain is 2 octaves of anti-aliased value noise, not fbm's 3. About 44 sin calls up close (-45%) and 8 far away (-90%). A/B (5 interleaved runs, llvmpipe, --bench 8 in an all-cobblestone room): 36-38 -> 50-52 FPS average, 1% low 25-30 -> 40-41, 0.1% low 19-28 -> 30-37. Screenshot diff at the same camera: mean 0.46 / 255, 0.15% of pixels over 16. q's desktop is the real test (F3 0.1% low on Cobblestone).
  • quark_maptool bots: the result has powerPools [A, B] in power mode. Used to check "power bots favour team A" (1.20 list C): 60 three-on-three matches on Reactors, Sewer, Amphitheatre and Bridge, 240 s each: A ahead 32, B 28. No bias, so no change.
  • World pass without discard while no holes are blown. The world shader has two discards: holes in thin breakable walls, and the see-through pass's invisible pixels. A shader with discard makes the GPU run it before the depth test, so every hidden pixel behind a nearer wall gets shaded. Renderer::buildWorldFast compiles a second world program without them (compileWorld(..., noDiscard)), and drawScene uses it for the opaque pass while holeCount_ is 0. The frame's own uniforms are set on both programs in prepareScene / drawScene; the rest (looks, grade, fog, shadow size, render scale, clean surfaces) are copied from worldProg_ by syncWorldFast only after a setter marks them stale (worldFastDirty_). With holes blown, and in the block and see-through passes, the old program draws as before.
    • Desktop A/B of the shipped code (3090 Ti, Hamlet --benchmark 2560x1440 fullscreen ULTRA, 6 interleaved rounds, QUARK_WORLD_DISCARD=1 against the default): 3561 to 3696 average FPS (+3.8%), 1% low 2848 to 3117 (+9.4%), 0.1% low 2589 to 2881 (+11.3%), every round. Tracy: GPU world pass 177.5 to 129.0 µs (-27%), CPU world pass unchanged (65.7 / 64.8 µs), the full uniform copy runs 3 times, all at map load. The screenshots are pixel-identical, and a hole blown in a brick wall stays see-through with no flicker (a test map on the desktop).
    • Two earlier versions lost 6% average FPS: reading the uniforms back each frame (glGetUniform*) cost 83 µs of CPU. The first one copied every frame by design, and the second one copied every frame by mistake, because buildCleanMeans (called every frame with PATTERN FADE on) marked the copy stale before its early return.
    • The other candidates tested in the same run: nearest-chunks-first (+2.6% avg, but coplanar faces flicker as the camera moves) and a depth-only prepass (-29% GPU world pass, but flat average FPS at ~3500 FPS). Neither ships.
    • QUARK_WORLD_DISCARD=1 turns the second program off, for A/B, and so does the LATENCY tab's new EARLY DEPTH TEST switch (fast_world, on by default; F3's tweaks line shows EARLY DEPTH), added so the owner can A/B it in game (owner, 2026-11-03: "keep the option i want to test it myself").
  • Russian LIGHTMAP FILTER label clipped at 1280x720: its values were "ЛАЙТМАП: ГЛАДКИЙ" / "ЛАЙТМАП: РЕЗКИЙ", repeating the label's subject; now just ГЛАДКИЙ / РЕЗКИЙ (the UI test's layout check).
  • Small per-frame savings from a code audit: the last per-frame glGetUniformLocation (uWorldEdge) is cached, the CLEAN SURFACES key is a std::array instead of a heap-allocated vector each frame, and the frame-time ring wraps with a mask instead of a 64-bit divide.
  • Build flags measured (desktop, interleaved): -O2 against -O3 + LTO + AVX2 is within 0.1% (GPU-bound). LOW LATENCY ON costs 3.3% average FPS but has the best lows (+2% vs OFF); ULTRA costs 8.2% average.
  • tools/lang/extract.py skips static_assert messages, which are never drawn.
  • Frame walkthrough (owner, 2026-11-02: "build a document that shows the code, pseudo code, and instructions for a single frame end to end"): every stage of a frame from the desktop Tracy captures (1.19.7, 3090 Ti, 1440p, ULTRA) with code, pseudo code, the hot machine code, what it does and why, and ranked fixes. The frame is GPU-bound (GPU world pass 176 of ~290 µs); the CPU's real work is ~167 µs (bench) / ~128 µs (FFA), the rest waiting on the GPU. Every big spike in the captures (1.6 s, 6.7 ms, 3.6 ms, 1.9 ms) was during a map load; the worst in-play frame was ~0.7 ms. The items below came out of it. Desktop A/B of all of them together (Hamlet --benchmark, 6 interleaved rounds, base b332895 against 766e6f4): 3561 to 3806 average FPS (+6.85%), 1% low 2839 to 2952 (+3.98%), 0.1% low 2580 to 2684 (+4.02%), every round; Tracy: prepareScene 26.3 to 13.6 µs, CPU shadow pass 11.3 to 3.9 µs, drawWorld 149.7 to 123.5 µs, Game::frame 178.6 to ~138 µs; the GPU world pass is unchanged (176 µs), so the frame is even more GPU-bound. The early depth test above was not in either side and stacks.
    • Shadow caster rect from bounding spheres. prepareScene used to put every caster triangle (4,539 vertices on the benchmark, reading 12 of each 72 bytes) through the shadow matrix to find the part of the shadow map casters can touch. box, boxPitched, mesh and the editor's dynamic slope now record a centre and radius as they emit, and the rect is the union of their circles (radius × the light rows' length). It is never smaller than the old rect (2,000 random scenes: always contains it, 1.5% larger on average). Micro-benchmark: 25-31 µs to 1.3 µs a frame at 8 players, 75-94 to 4-5 µs at 24. Screenshots identical.
    • 44-byte dynamic vertex (was 72). Positions stay float; the normal is three shorts, colour + sun visibility, edge distances and outline colour + width are half floats (the edge now stores the distance to all four edges and the vertex shader rebuilds the old form, so halfs lose nothing on a slope's 1000 m "no edge"). emitBox packs a box's values once and writes every vertex in place (building a vertex on the stack and copying it was slower: store-forwarding stalls); DynVertex no longer zero-fills when the lists grow. Meshes use SSE2 for the packing (scalar fallback for arm64). Box emission 240 to 155 ns, the upload 405 to 248 KB a frame at 8 players. Trade-off: colours quantize to within 2 of 255 levels on 0.7% of pixels (player faces, the view model); outlines unchanged.
    • The dynamic buffer is orphaned at the bytes written (rounded up to 64 KB) instead of the whole grown capacity. Pre-sizing it at init and orphaning it whole measured slower (632 against 592 µs prepareScene on software GL); bytes-written measured 566.
    • Shadow pass scissored to the caster rect (+3 texels a side, more than the widest filter reaches). The GPU shadow pass cost a flat 16.4 µs whatever the casters, i.e. the full 2048² clear; nothing outside the rect is ever sampled.
    • Stuck props: each prop's model yaw and pitch (atan2 + asin) are worked out once when it lands (readPropEvent) instead of every frame; bit-identical (a test compares them). drawStuckProps was 10 µs a frame on the benchmark.
    • Collision grid compact references. Beside cellBrushes the grid keeps cellRefs (28-byte GridRef: the brush's box, shape, a stops-players / shots / sight / clip / wallbang / glass bit set, and its index), written by buildGrid and changeBrush (the only two writers). Sweeps (forBox) and rays (raycast, raycastBullet, castAny, raycastAnyHinted) no longer load a 148-byte Brush per reference just to read its material; a Brush is read only for a slope's planes or a hit's material. Results are bit-identical: 11.9 M queries on all 62 maps (rays at body heights with and without clip, sweeps on 3 axes at 4 scales, boxSolid, again after breaking glass, blocks and holes) compared old against new, simhash and the ISA check unchanged. Rays 14-32% faster, sweeps 2-25%, a server tick ~4% (serverbench). A test checks the refs stay in step after random edits and destruction. Trade-off: 28 bytes per grid reference (~330 KB on hamlet).
    • Server game log on its own thread. --log lines are formatted on the tick thread into a reused buffer and handed to an AsyncFileWriter (as the demo recorder) every 256 KB or 1 s; before, a 1 MB stdio buffer was written inside whichever tick filled it. Same bytes (md5 of a 23 MB extreme log). Against a disk draining at 8 MB/s the worst tick fell from 5-97 ms to 1.5-6 ms. Trade-off: if the disk stalls, the waiting log grows in memory.
    • Loading screen paced to a 33 ms deadline (sleepPrecise, waking as soon as the bake ends) instead of 30 × sleepMs(1), which takes ~2 ms each on Windows: frames went from ~60 ms (16 FPS) to ~35 ms.
    • Renderer warmup covers more first-use driver work: the late-latch buffer is created at load (it was created on the first frame in play, latch on or off), and the warmup draws two frames, the second with the view model faded and its translucent parts. The first-frames view-pass spike went from ~1.6 to ~0.33 ms on software GL. Trade-off: one more warmup frame per load, behind the loading screen.
    • Bots: the route searches' scratch is sized when the nav graph is built (in the background), and the build thread frees its own copy of the map. The first bot think on a map went from 75 to 41 µs; decisions unchanged (simhash identical).
    • Profiling builds: the stderr stats line printed every 5 s from flush2D is gone (it made a 0.5 ms frame every 5 s in Tracy captures, 215 ms on the desktop's console); its values are Tracy plots now.
  • Clean-surface averages on the GPU (frame walkthrough: the readback was a 4.5-6 ms CPU wait at each load, and again on every frame a palette setting changed). Each look's patches go on a 64 px grid of one RGBA32F texture, glGenerateMipmap level 6 holds one texel per patch (its mean), and one glBlitFramebuffer per look copies them into uCleanTex; the world shader is unchanged and nothing waits on glReadPixels. Latency

  • Mouse input on its own restricted thread (owner, 2026-11-03: "mouse input own its own restricted thread is most important"). Raw input already had its own thread (HIGHEST priority, any core). Now, with the new setting MOUSE THREAD: OWN CORE (on by default, config mouse_own_core; MORE 9 under FRAME CAP OFFSET), that thread:
    • runs at THREAD_PRIORITY_TIME_CRITICAL, above the game's HIGHEST, so a report preempts anything else of ours;
    • is pinned (hard affinity) to one logical core: the last one of the fastest efficiency class (GetSystemCpuSetInformation; on Intel hybrid CPUs that's a P-core, never an E-core; parked cores skipped); on older Windows, the last core;
    • is never power-throttled (ThreadPowerThrottling, execution speed);
    • keeps the game's own thread off that core (its affinity minus that core, given back when the thread stops or the setting goes off). Why: an 8 kHz mouse sends a report every 125 µs. On a shared core a report could wait for the game's frame or another thread's time slice before it was even summed, so the frame's mouse read could miss motion that had already arrived. On its own core the thread wakes as each report lands. The cost is one logical core the game's main thread no longer uses. It isn't pinned with fewer than 4 cores, or on Linux / macOS (GLFW's raw motion there). SHARED is the old behaviour, for A/B. The hint shows which core it's on.

  • F3's swap-to-GPU gap sawtooth (owner, 2026-11-03: "the wait between swap and GPU slowly grows over 10 seconds, then drops back down"). The two clocks tick at slightly different rates, so between calibrations every GPU time slid by the drift, then snapped back at the next one. It was measurement only: the frames never changed. The game now keeps the drift rate from the last two calibrations and places each GPU time with offset + drift * (time since the calibration) (Game::gpuToCpu). Netcode and server

  • NET-14: projectile ids never repeat while one is in flight. newProjId skips 0 and any id a live projectile holds, so a long-lived sticky never shares its id after the u16 wrap (a wrong trail or missed explosion). Server only, no protocol change; one scan of 128 slots per shot.
  • COR-8: interpolation is clamped to kMaxInterpMs + 0.1 s behind the newest snapshot, and the bracket search stops at a server clock reset; it could draw poses up to ~1 s old.
  • Visibility rays scale with the target's size (a MOVE_BIG giant's head is twice as high), so a giant whose head sticks out over cover is sent. Same ray budget.
  • breakGlass's shard scratch list is a heap buffer that is never freed instead of a thread_local object: a thread_local with a destructor crashed at thread exit under MinGW on hosted servers. Still no allocation per break.
  • install.sh keeps a testing install that is ahead of the checkout, runs the testing updater during the install, and sets StartLimitIntervalSec=0 on the server units. Tutorial (1.19.9 "Tutorial update"; q: "show a demo of a bot doing whatever movement tech, then puts you on a map that shows the bot doing it and gives you instructions on how to, all the special moves and combos")

  • What it is. MAIN MENU > PRACTICE > TUTORIAL (or quark --tutorial [LESSON[:PHASE]]) hosts a local game like TIME TRIAL (Game::startTutorial -> startHost with ServerConfig::tutorial): the map tutorial, FFA, three players (you, the GHOST and, in the two grapple-a-player lessons, the PARTNER), warmup for good (autoStart off: no clock, no scores). Each lesson has a DEMO and a TRY phase:
    • DEMO: the camera follows the ghost from behind and over your lane. Your input is held. The ghost's keys light up under your own binds' names (W / A / S / D, JUMP, DASH, GRAPPLE, FIRE, ALT FIRE), with where it aims (level, a little down, straight down, up) and its turning. SUPER / HYPER pop up over it as it does them. JUMP, ENTER or a click hands over to you; otherwise one loop of the script plays.
    • TRY: you at your lane's start, the ghost looping beside you. The steps are on the left and the goal has a bar and a number. The lesson's check runs on your own predicted movement every frame, so the bar moves as you see yourself move. Done: LESSON DONE, NEXT: <lesson>, and 2.5 s later the next lesson. After the last: TUTORIAL COMPLETE (LESSONS, MODES GUIDE: INFO > MODES, PLAY VS BOTS).

Esc: RESUME, WATCH AGAIN, SKIP LESSON, START OVER, LESSONS, SETTINGS, LEAVE TUTORIAL, QUIT. LESSONS lists all 28 in four columns (three first, but ten KEY TECH lessons pushed BACK off a 720p screen), the ten KEY TECH ones first under a gold KEY TECH heading (a gold bar on each), and a green mark on each one done (tutorial_done, a bit per lesson, kept across sessions; an int whose limit is 2^30, so static_assert(L_COUNT <= 30)).

Existing players see it once too, since their config has no such key yet. It isn't shown in the UI test or screenshot runs (--tutorial-offer forces it for screenshots).
